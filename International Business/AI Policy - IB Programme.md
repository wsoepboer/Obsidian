# AI Policy — IB Programme

Programme-wide GenAI policy. [[Welcome|↩ Vault home]] · [[International Business - Programme Info|Programme Info]]

> [!info] Source
> The module **"Use of GenAI within the IB programme"**, published identically in every IB Brightspace course — captured 3 September 2026 from *Global Sales and Accountmanagement (26/27)* (`201011`) and *Global Dynamics and Supply Chain Management (26/27)* (`200779`). This is boilerplate: the same text appears in each unit, so it is filed once here rather than per course.

## The position, in the programme's own words
> First of all, ChatGPT and other GenAI will help you become even better as an international business professional, **we encourage their use**. Even more, it is important that the programme prepares you for the responsible use of GenAI in the future professional field. At the same time, their use can intentionally or unintentionally lead to **fraud**.

Encouraged, and fraud-adjacent. Both halves are the policy.

## The one hard requirement
> When you make use of GenAI tools it is **mandatory to complete the AI statement**, mention what tools you have used and add the statement(s) to your work.

Three obligations, all mechanical:

1. **Complete the AI statement** — the Windesheim form
2. **Name the tools** you used
3. **Attach the statement(s) to your work**

> [!danger] This is a pass/fail gate, not a formality
> Both unit rubrics list *"The declaration on the use of GenAI has been completed"* among the **conditional criteria**. Miss a conditional criterion and the maximum possible grade is a **near pass (5)**, however good the work is.

## Practical reading
- **Declare as you go.** The statement asks which tools were used for what; that is easy to write during the work and guesswork afterwards.
- **Keep a prompt log.** Not required by this text, but it is what makes a declaration verifiable rather than a claim — and the same habit is required outright by the [[AI Policy|Family Business & Research AI policy]], which is far stricter.
- **Tool, not source.** Anything a model asserts still needs a real, citable source; the referencing standard is a separate conditional criterion.
- **Fabricated data is the line.** The word this module uses is *fraud*, and the EER's Chapter 6 is where the consequences are defined — they are not grade-level.
