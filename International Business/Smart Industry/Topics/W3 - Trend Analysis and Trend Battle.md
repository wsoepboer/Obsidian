# W3 — Trend Analysis & Trend Battle

[[Smart Industry - Course Info|↩ Course]] · **Prev:** [[W2 - Industry and Organisation Analysis]] · **Next:** [[W4 - Formative Assessment Pitch]] · [Brightspace](https://leren.windesheim.nl/d2l/le/lessons/203043/lessons/1698474)

> The kick-off deck calls this week **Trend Analysis & Trend lab**, and places the **assessment of the trend analysis** here rather than in week 4. See the warning in [[Assessment & Deadlines]].

## Before
- Finish [[Porter's Five Forces]] for your industry; you can characterise its competitive environment.
- Research the **HR function** at two levels:
  - **INDUSTRY (meso)** — how has it changed over the **last 50 years** because of trends, and how will it change over the **next 3-5 years**?
  - **ORGANISATION (micro)** — the same question for your company.
- Bring a list of influential trends in your industry to class.

## During
- The most impactful trends and how they relate to technology adoption, across industries and inside business operations.
- Identify the most impactful trends for your industry, your organisation and its HRM.
- Decide **which single trend is most impactful** — the conclusion of part 1.

## Week 3 deliverable — trend analysis
1. **Trend overview** — the main trends you identified in your chosen category, showing you explored multiple directions before choosing.
2. **Select one trend** to carry forward, relevant for your company, the industry, and future customers and markets. This becomes the core trend of the Show & Tell advice.
3. **Trend or hype?** — argue clearly why it is a real trend and not a short-term hype, **supported by models and sources** (Rogers, Gartner). → [[Trend Classification]] · [[Gartner Hype Cycle]] · [[Diffusion of Innovation]]
4. **Why this trend over the others** — research and data, strong arguments, clear opportunities for the company, strategic fit with its capabilities and positioning, relevance for the industry's future.

![[slide-59.jpg]]

> [!important] What counts as a trend
> **AI is not a trend.** Too unspecific, and its applications are already omnipresent. Focus on phenomena **not yet adopted by the majority**. Once almost everyone has adopted something, it is no longer a trend.
>
> The lecturer's working distinction: a **hype** rises fast on buzz and fades just as fast; a **trend** develops gradually, reflects a deeper shift in behaviour, technology, culture or society, and has lasting influence.

## After
- The most impactful trends identified, with their effects on industry, organisation, consumer and society.
- You start building the visual slide deck: industry analysis and classification, company analysis, trend analysis, conclusion.

## Self-test
- Give three phenomena in your industry and argue for each: hype, trend, or adopted norm. Use Rogers *and* Gartner.
- How has HR in your industry changed since ~1976? Three shifts and their driver.
- Which trend is most impactful for your organisation, and what is the strongest counter-argument?
- Restate your conclusion in Pyramid form: situation, complication, question, answer — then three arguments, then the data under each.
