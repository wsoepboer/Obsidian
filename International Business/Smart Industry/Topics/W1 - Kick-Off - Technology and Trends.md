## During — what the deck actually covers

### 1. What is technology?
Asked without an encyclopaedia. The deck's point, via Aristotle, Schatzberg and Salomon: the German pair **Technik** (the technical activity itself) vs **Technologie** (the academic principle) does not exist in English, which is why "technology" is used loosely. After the Industrial Revolution the word came to mean all technical activity applying science to practical ends.

**Technology is always a result of the application of our creative thinking — and it led to long-term economic growth.** That sentence is the spine of the lecture.

### 2. History of technology
Walked through as a chain of firsts: **stone tools** (~2 Mya, hammering flakes off a pebble; refined into pressure flaking ~75 kya) · **cooking** (the cooking hypothesis: cooking grew the hominid brain, evidence contested) · **agriculture** · **clothing** · **sailing** · **pottery** · **the wheel** · **electricity** · **the telegraph** · and forward to **neural interfaces**, human-machine fusion, quantum computing, self-regulating systems and G-AI ("singularity is near?").

### 3. Kondratieff waves
Mapped with Grinin & Grinin's sixth-wave / Cybernetic Revolution model → [[Kondratieff Waves]]. The lecturer's question on the slide: **what does this mean for management, thinking HR?**

### 4. Adoption of technology and trends
Worked through the **self-checkout** as the running case: launched in the Netherlands in **2005**. Smart carts, robotic convenience stores, "which do you prefer, A or B". Then the models:
- [[Diffusion of Innovation]] — Rogers, plus the five constructs that decide adoption.
- [[Gartner Hype Cycle]] — used for the trend analysis.
- **Hype or trend?** → [[Trend Classification]].
- Effects of the self-checkout on **society, the business, the customer** (theft, loneliness, firing vs training staff, understaffing, shorter queues).

### 5. Why companies must innovate digitally
Stay relevant to existing customers whose demands rise · serve new segments, markets, products or business models · differentiate from competitors · improve efficiency and cut cost · **stay alive**. Cases of firms that did not adapt: **V&D, Sears, Kodak, Thomas Cook**.

Where the opportunities are, per **Nylén & Holmström** → [[Digital Innovation Strategy]]: product dimension (user experience, value proposition), environmental dimension (digital evolution scanning: new devices, channels, behaviours), organisational dimension (skills, improvisation — e.g. Google skunkworks / Area 120).

Also covered: **digitalisation of society and retail** and the birth of the "merciless consumer" — price transparency, customer in control, ever higher expectations, extreme ease expected, less loyal.

## Week 1 deliverable — industry analysis
1. **Define the industry type** — what industry, core activities and value creation, primary customer groups. Analytical, not descriptive.
2. **Industry structure** — fragmented vs concentrated, types of firms (start-ups, SMEs, large), dominant business models, industry timeline.
3. **Quantitative insights** — at least **five** meaningful statistics: market size, number of firms, employment size, growth over the past 3-5 years, forecast growth, export share, technology investment or digital adoption level. For each: the number, the source, and what it means for the industry.
4. **Industry Technology & Competitiveness Scan** — the [[Industry Technology and Competitiveness Scan|Porter worksheet]].
5. **Concluding insight** — mature, growing or declining? How technology-driven? How would you divide the industry using Rogers' diffusion? Single biggest technology opportunity? Single biggest risk? **Support with data and scores, not opinion.**
