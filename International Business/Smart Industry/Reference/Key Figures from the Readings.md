# Key Figures from the Readings

The rubric grades **quantitative data used appropriately**, with the number, the source and what it means. These are the headline figures from the course literature, ready to cite. [[Smart Industry - Course Info|↩ Course]] · [[Literature and Sources]]

## PwC — *Sizing the prize* (marked "must use" in the filename)
- Global GDP could be up to **+14% higher in 2030** because of AI — an additional **$15.7 trillion**, described as the biggest commercial opportunity in today's economy.
- Largest gains: **China up to +26% of GDP by 2030**, **North America +14%**.
- Biggest sector gains: **retail, financial services, healthcare**, through productivity, product quality and consumption.
- PwC's four ways AI works, from human-in-the-loop to no-human-in-the-loop: **automated, assisted, augmented, autonomous intelligence**. Automated intelligence = automation of manual/cognitive and routine/non-routine tasks.

## WEF — *Future of Jobs Report 2025* (January 2025)
Based on **over 1,000 employers**, **14 million workers**, **22 industry clusters**, **55 economies**, horizon **2025-2030**.

- Structural churn equals **22% of today's total jobs**: **+14% created (170 million)** offset by **-8% displaced (92 million)**, net **+7% (78 million)**.
- Most transformative trend: **broadening digital access**, named by **60%** of employers. Then **AI and information processing (86% of technology-related responses)**, **robotics and automation (58%)**, **energy generation, storage and distribution (41%)**.
- **Increasing cost of living** is the second most transformative trend overall (**50%**); general economic slowdown **42%**, expected to displace **1.6 million** jobs.
- **Climate-change mitigation** third (**47%**), adaptation sixth (**41%**).
- **Geoeconomic fragmentation** drives business-model change in **34%** of organisations.
- **39%** of workers' existing skill sets will be transformed or outdated by 2030 — down from 44% in 2023 and 57% in 2020.
- Of every 100 workers, **59 need training by 2030**: 29 upskilled in role, 19 upskilled and redeployed, **11 unlikely to get it at all**.
- **63%** of employers name **skill gaps** as the biggest barrier to transformation. **85%** will prioritise upskilling, **70%** will hire for new skills, **50%** will move staff from declining to growing roles, **40%** will cut staff where skills lose relevance.
- Fastest-**growing** skills: **AI and big data**, networks and cybersecurity, technological literacy; then creative thinking, resilience/flexibility/agility, curiosity and lifelong learning. Most sought-after core skill: **analytical thinking (7 in 10 employers)**. Declining: manual dexterity, endurance, precision (**24%** see a decrease).
- Fastest-growing **roles** in percentage terms: big data specialists, fintech engineers, AI and ML specialists, software developers; plus green roles (EV specialists, environmental and renewable-energy engineers). Largest absolute growth: farmworkers, delivery drivers, construction workers, salespersons, food processing, and care and education roles.
- Fastest-**declining**: clerical and secretarial — cashiers and ticket clerks, administrative assistants and executive secretaries, postal clerks, bank tellers, data entry clerks.
- **Half of employers plan to re-orient the business around AI**, two-thirds will hire AI skills, **40% expect to reduce workforce where AI can automate tasks**.
- **64%** name supporting employee health and well-being as a key talent-attraction strategy. DEI initiatives at **83%** of employers, up from 67% in 2023.

## Brynjolfsson & McAfee — *The Second Machine Age* (2014)
- The **first** machine age was the Industrial Revolution, whose key technology, the steam engine, **overcame the limits of human and animal muscle power** and produced factories, mass production and railways.
- The **second** machine age does the same for **mental power**: "computers and other digital advances are doing for mental power what the steam engine and its descendants did for muscle power".
- Three observations: **rapid progress ahead** (we are at an inflection point where the curve bends), **profound benefit** (more variety, more volume, more choice), and **tough challenges** — chiefly growing inequality.
- Summary review: Marien, M. (2014). *Cadmus*, 2(2), 174.
