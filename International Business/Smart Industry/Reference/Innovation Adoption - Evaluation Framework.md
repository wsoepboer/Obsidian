## 1. Strategic fit
| Question | What a good answer looks like |
|---|---|
| Does the innovation fit **the industry/area**? | Named industry characteristics from your Week 2 analysis — regulation, margin structure, capital intensity — and why the trend does or doesn't suit them |
| Who is the **target audience**? | Segment, not "customers". Ties to STP from Week 2 |
| Who are the **stakeholders**? | Internal (staff, management, works council) and external (customers, regulators, suppliers, community). Map by influence and interest |
| **Full replacement or partial**? | Whether it substitutes an existing process entirely or augments it. Determines the size of the change-management problem |

---

## 2. People & organisation — the HRM core
| Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Can current personnel use it?** | Existing skill baseline vs required skill. Be specific about which roles |
| **Schooling / training required?** | Duration, cost, who delivers it, productivity dip during the learning curve |
| **Do existing tasks change?** | Which tasks are automated away, which are redefined, which are unaffected |
| **Do new tasks appear?** | New roles created — prompt engineering, data stewardship, model monitoring, maintenance |
| **Culture fit?** | Does the organisation have the risk appetite, digital maturity and management style this requires? Where is resistance likely, and from whom? |

> [!tip] Connect to Rogers here
> Your workforce contains the full adopter distribution. Identify your internal **early adopters** — they are your implementation lever. Also identify which **stage of the innovation-decision process** staff are in, because the intervention differs by stage. See [[Rogers - Diffusion of Innovations]].

---

## 3. Financial
| Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Costs** | Acquisition/licensing, implementation, training, ongoing operation, maintenance, opportunity cost |
| **ROI — monetary** | Payback period, cost saved, revenue enabled. Say the assumptions out loud |
| **ROI — non-monetary** | Employee retention, safety, compliance position, quality, customer satisfaction, sustainability, employer brand. Often the stronger argument in HRM framing |

---

## 4. Risk vs upside
| Side | Cover |
|---|---|
| **Upside** | Competitive advantage, first-mover position, efficiency, capability |
| **Risk — operational** | Failure modes, vendor dependency, integration debt |
| **Risk — people** | Resistance, attrition of staff who feel displaced, morale |
| **Risk — external** | Regulatory (AI Act, GDPR), reputational, societal |
| **Risk of *not* acting** | Frequently omitted and frequently the strongest argument. What happens if a competitor adopts and you don't? |

---

## 5. Time & rollout
| Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| **How long until company-wide?** | Realistic phased timeline, not a single launch date |
| **Rollout approach** | Pilot → department → company-wide. Pilot design maps directly to Rogers' **trialability** |
| **Milestones and checkpoints** | What would make you stop or reverse |
| **Time-to-plateau of the trend itself** | From [[Gartner Hype Cycle]] — if the trend is 5-10 years from the plateau, a full rollout now is a different bet than a pilot |

### What the rubric demands that this framework does not yet cover
Add these five, they carry marks:

1. **The three levels as an explicit organising axis.** Every piece of advice must be placed at **strategic, tactical or operational** level. The rubric says it twice — once for the advice, once for the impact reasoning.
2. **Impact reasoning, separately from the advice.** "The impact of following this advice is thoroughly and critically reasoned" at all three levels. Advice and consequence-of-advice are two different sections.
3. **Explicit link back to the analysis.** The HR advice must visibly follow from your Part 1 industry and organisation work, not stand alone.
4. **Long-term organisational objectives.** "Strategic implications are formulated explicitly, with clear connections to long-term organisational objectives."
5. **International sources, and a rich variety of theories and models, justified.** Not just applied — **justified**. This is the difference between good and excellent across every criterion in the rubric.

Also remember the two prerequisites that cap the grade at 5 regardless of content: **APA with sources on the slides** and a bibliography mapping sources to parts, and **additional assignments if you missed workshops**.
