# Company & Sub-Sector Selection — Deep Research

[[Smart Industry - Course Info|↩ Course]] · Compiled 2026-09-11, ahead of the 19 Sep 2026 hand-in

Group notes said film industry, uncertain on fit/data. This compares candidates across five sub-sectors against what the rubric actually needs: 4-level GICS fit, a ~50-year quantitative trend with a live (not resolved) future state, a real analyzable workforce for the IN/THROUGH/OUT employment-cycle argument in Part 2, and genuine data accessibility by the 19 Sep deadline. Full rubric detail: [[Assessment & Deadlines]].

## Ranked shortlist (best candidates across all sub-sectors)

### 1. Wētā FX — VFX & Animation — top pick
**Data:** Private (NZ, founder-owned) but discloses financials to NZ's Companies Office. FY2025 revenue NZ$478m (FY2024: NZ$430m), FY2025 loss NZ$59m (FY2024 loss: NZ$82m), regional revenue split (NZ $411m, Canada $34m, Australia $25m, US $7.3m), not profitable until 2027-28 per own projections.
**GICS:** Communication Services → Media & Entertainment → Entertainment → Movies & Entertainment.
**Trend:** CGI/VFX tool adoption over ~50 years, sharpened by its own 2021 sale of its software/tools division to Unity for $2.3bn (pivot back to pure services) — a distinctive, quotable inflection point, not a generic "AI disrupts VFX" claim.
**HR story (strongest of all candidates):** ~2,200 staff; 265 redundancies in 2023 after the Unity deal collapsed; a live Aug–Sept 2025 restructuring proposing ~100 further cuts (production, marketing, finance, IT, recruitment), reported with dates and headcounts by Newsroom NZ, NZ Herald, BusinessDesk, HCAmag. Explicitly tied to post-strike/AI-driven demand slowdown.
**Risk:** private, so no analyst-grade detail — mitigate via NZ Companies Office filings + the dense 2023-2025 press trail.

### 2. Crunchyroll — niche streaming — strongest HR narrative, most original
**Data:** Private Sony subsidiary, but Sony discloses subscriber KPIs at the Sony Pictures Entertainment segment level (21M subscribers FY2025/26, up from ~5M in 2018). Crunchyroll Japan additionally files standalone local financials (net profit down 63% in 2025 despite subscriber growth) — a rare combo of parent-level + standalone-subsidiary numbers.
**GICS:** Communication Services → Media & Entertainment → Entertainment → most defensibly Movies & Entertainment (borders Interactive Media & Services — worth stating and justifying both).
**Trend:** Clean 50-year arc — bootleg VHS fansubs (1980s-90s) → internet fansub piracy (2000s) → legal simulcast streaming (2006/2009) → current AI-assisted localization, with two decades of hard subscriber-growth data points.
**HR story:** July 2025 AI-subtitle controversy (ChatGPT credit found embedded in an episode) → Aug 12, 2025 layoffs and restructuring into regional engineering hubs (US/Mexico/India) → CEO's public pledge (May 2026) not to use AI for subs/dubs. A dated, documented IN/THROUGH/OUT case with real job postings to ground the STP/HR sections.
**Note:** genuinely novel pick — unlikely to overlap with other groups, unlike Netflix.

### 3. ZOO Digital Group plc — post-production/localization — safest data, ties to your "language industry" interest
**Data:** Genuinely public (LSE AIM: ZOO) — full annual reports, RNS filings via Investegate, analyst coverage. FY2026 revenue $42.3M (down 14.7% YoY), Adjusted EBITDA $4.0M (from $1.1M), operating loss narrowed to $1.6M. Restructuring explicitly disclosed: headcount cuts in US/UK, $14.4M of annual fixed costs cut across FY25-26.
**GICS:** Communication Services → Media, Entertainment & Interactive → Interactive Media & Services (or Media — a genuine classification judgment call, which itself satisfies the rubric's "justify the fit" requirement).
**Trend:** Human dubbing/subtitling → AI-driven localization. ZOO runs ZOOdubs (cloud dubbing platform since 2017) and published an Oct 2024 AI White Paper on human-in-the-loop AI for transcription/translation/script prep/voice recording.
**HR story:** 500+ staff plus a network of 5,000+ freelance translators/dubbing artists; grew via Netflix Preferred Vendor status (2016) and Disney/Sony contracts, contracted amid the 2023 strikes and streamer spend pullback, now restructuring while pushing AI.
**Risk:** small-cap, no dedicated HR/ESG report — the "HR function" section needs inference from job postings + annual report narrative.
**Bridges your original "language industry" idea** to an actual, fully public, GICS-classifiable company.

### 4. Lionsgate Studios Corp. — independent/mid-size production — the safe fallback if you want zero data risk
**Data:** Fully public (NASDAQ: LION), SEC 10-K/10-Q. FY2026 10-K discloses 916 full-time employees, segment revenue split (Motion Picture 60.8% / TV 39.2%), customer concentration (18% of revenue from Amazon), explicit risk-factor language on AI and labor disputes/strikes. Market cap ~$1.66B.
**GICS:** Communication Services → Media & Entertainment → Movies and Entertainment.
**Trend:** Industry-wide shift from theatrical-first to hybrid theatrical/streaming/library-licensing, sharpened by its own 2024 SPAC merger (post-Starz spin-off) into a pure-play studio.
**HR story:** real but less dramatic than #1-3 — disclosed headcount and strike/AI risk language, but no single dated crisis event found yet.
**Use case:** pick this if your group wants to eliminate data risk entirely, at the cost of a slightly less distinctive trend story.

## Also assessed, not recommended as primary pick
- **DNEG** (LSE-listed VFX) — genuinely public, but this pass only reached secondary aggregators; would need a direct pull of DNEG's own RNS/annual report for workforce specifics before it could compete with Wētā FX.
- **CuriosityStream** (NASDAQ: CURI) — excellent financial transparency (revenue +46% YoY, EBITDA positive) but weak HR depth and only founded 2015, thinning the 50-year framing.
- **Iyuno** (dubbing market leader) — richest trend narrative of all localization candidates but wholly private/PE-backed, zero public financials — usable only as market-context comparator, not as the analyzed company.
- **Blumhouse** — strong press-based numbers ($1bn 2025 box office, $5bn lifetime) but no disclosed financials or headcount, only scraped estimates.
- **A24, Legendary** — high originality, essentially no quantitative HR/workforce data (A24: $3.5B valuation via funding rounds only; Legendary: Apollo-owned since a $760M investment, no segment-level HR disclosure).
- **Cinema exhibition (AMC/Cinemark)** — already assessed in an earlier pass: the obvious "premiumization" trend is confirmed as a well-established, industry-wide pattern (Cinemark/Marcus run the identical playbook), and no workforce/HR detail could be found tied to it. Weak on both originality and Part 2 depth.
- **IMAX** — good public data (NYSE: IMAX) but its laser-projection story is "operational optimization, not innovation," and its asset-light licensing model means a small, non-representative workforce — thin material for the employment-cycle argument.

## Bottom line
Wētā FX, Crunchyroll and ZOO Digital are the three strongest picks overall — each pairs a real, dated HR event with defensible quantitative data and avoids the "everyone converges on the same solution" trap Boris warned about. Wētā FX has the deepest HR-crisis paper trail; Crunchyroll is the most original and has the cleanest 50-year arc; ZOO Digital is the only one of the three with full statutory public-company disclosure, and it's the one that also connects to the language/localization angle the group found interesting early on. Lionsgate is the right call only if the group wants to remove data risk entirely.
