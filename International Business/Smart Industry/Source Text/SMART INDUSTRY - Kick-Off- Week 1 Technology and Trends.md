A hype is something that becomes very popular very quickly because of excitement, marketing, or buzz, but it usually fades just as fast. It is short-lived, often driven by media, influencers, or novelty, and people talk about it a lot for a brief period without it necessarily leading to lasting change.

A trend, on the other hand, develops more gradually and lasts longer. It reflects a deeper shift in behavior, preferences, technology, culture, or society. Trends influence how people think or act over time and can have a more permanent impact.
In short:

Hype: fast rise, lots of buzz, short duration.
Trend: slower development, broader adoption, long-term influence.

"Skunkworks" is a term often used to describe small, highly autonomous teams within a larger organization that work on advanced or experimental projects. At Google, a Skunkworks team typically works on innovative ideas that are separate from the main product lines, with minimal bureaucracy. These teams have the freedom to experiment, fail fast, and iterate quickly, often aiming to create breakthrough technologies or products that might not fit into Google’s existing portfolio.

Area 120 is Google’s internal incubator for experimental projects and startups. It allows employees to work on their own ideas for about 20% of their time, with support from Google in terms of resources and mentorship. The goal of Area 120 is to nurture innovative products that could eventually become part of Google’s official offerings or even spin off into independent companies. Projects here are typically high-risk, high-reward, and operate with startup-like agility within the larger company.
