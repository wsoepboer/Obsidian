# Digital Innovation Strategy

**Nylén, D. & Holmström, J. (2014). "Digital innovation strategy: A framework for diagnosing and improving digital product and service innovation." *Business Horizons*.** Umeå University, Swedish Center for Digital Innovation. Open access. `Files/Digital_innovation_strategy_A_framework_for_diagno.pdf`

Digital technology restructures whole industries, and its innovation processes are hard to control or predict, so firms need **dynamic diagnostic tools** rather than fixed plans. The framework covers **five key areas across three dimensions**, plus a diagnostic tool for implementation.

![[slide-48.jpg]]

## Product dimension
- **User experience** — usability, engagement, segmentation. Example from class: self-scan in shops, digital collection of saving stamps.
- **Value proposition** — new propositions and repositioning in the value chain. Examples: Airbnb, Uber, PayPal, HelloFresh.

![[slide-49.jpg]]
![[slide-50.jpg]]
![[slide-51.jpg]]

## Environmental dimension
- **Digital evolution scanning** — watching for **new devices** (AirPods, smart speakers), **new channels** (marketplaces, Instagram and Facebook Shops) and **new behaviours** (sustainability, healthy eating).

![[slide-52.jpg]]

## Organisational dimension
- **Skills** — new roles and teams (the arrival of AI), continuous re-education for 21st-century skills.
- **Improvisation** — space, time and coordination for experimentation. Examples: Google skunkworks, Area 120.

![[slide-53.jpg]]

## Why firms innovate digitally (from the deck)
Stay relevant to customers whose demands keep rising · serve new segments, markets, products or business models · differentiate · improve efficiency and cut cost · **avoid being put out of business**. The cautionary cases: **V&D, Sears, Kodak, Thomas Cook**.
