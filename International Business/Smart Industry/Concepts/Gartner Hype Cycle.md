# Gartner Hype Cycle

**Course:** [[Smart Industry - Course Info|Smart Industry]]
**Related:** [[Rogers - Diffusion of Innovations]] · [[Innovation Adoption - Evaluation Framework]] · [[Trend Classification]]

---

## What it is
A branded graphical model from Gartner, introduced by Jackie Fenn in 1995. It plots **expectations about a technology against time**, arguing that new technologies follow a characteristic path of overexcitement, collapse, and eventual realistic productivity.

Crucially — and this is the distinction people get wrong — the y-axis is **expectations**, not adoption. It tracks sentiment and visibility, not how many people actually use the thing.

---

## The five phases
| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. Innovation Trigger** | A breakthrough, proof-of-concept or launch generates press interest. No usable products yet, commercial viability unproven. |
| **2. Peak of Inflated Expectations** | Publicity produces a wave of success stories and a larger number of failures. Expectations far exceed what the technology can deliver. |
| **3. Trough of Disillusionment** | Interest collapses as implementations fail to deliver. Providers consolidate or go under. Investment continues only where survivors improve the product for early adopters. |
| **4. Slope of Enlightenment** | Concrete benefits crystallise. Second- and third-generation products appear. More enterprises fund pilots; conservative organisations stay cautious. |
| **5. Plateau of Productivity** | Mainstream adoption begins. Viability criteria are clearly defined. Broad market applicability visibly pays off. |

## How it differs from Rogers — and why you want both
| | Gartner Hype Cycle | [[Rogers - Diffusion of Innovations]] |
|---|---|---|
| **Y-axis** | Expectations / visibility | Adoption (% of population) |
| **Measures** | Market sentiment, media attention | Actual behaviour |
| **Shape** | Peak, trough, plateau | S-curve (cumulative) |
| **Basis** | Gartner analyst judgement | Empirical sociological research |

The peak of hype **precedes** real adoption, often by years. A technology can sit at the Peak of Inflated Expectations while still at innovator stage on Rogers — enormous noise, almost no users.

> [!tip] Strong move for the trend analysis
> Plot your trend on **both**. If it sits at the hype peak but at innovator stage on Rogers, you have an argument: the attention is real, the adoption isn't, and your company is choosing between moving early and waiting for the Slope of Enlightenment. That is a strategic trade-off rather than a description — exactly the "arguments, not concepts" standard Boris set.

## How it is used in this course
The deck introduces it right after the self-checkout case, under **"is it a hype or a trend?"** Boris's own working distinction, from the speaker notes:

> A **hype** becomes very popular very quickly because of excitement, marketing or buzz, and fades just as fast — short-lived, driven by media, influencers or novelty, lots of talk without lasting change. A **trend** develops more gradually and lasts longer, reflecting a deeper shift in behaviour, preferences, technology, culture or society.

## Criticisms
Commercially influential, academically weak. Use it, but hedge it.

- **It isn't a cycle.** A single path with an endpoint; nothing returns to the start.
- **No published methodology.** Gartner does not disclose how technologies are positioned. Placement is analyst judgement, not measurement.
- **The y-axis is unquantified.** "Expectations" has no defined unit or instrument.
- **Retroactive fitting.** Positions are frequently revised after the fact, which makes the model hard to falsify.
- **Not universal.** Dedehayir & Steinert (2016) found many technologies don't follow the pattern — some go straight to productivity, some never leave the trough, some never generate hype at all.
- **One curve for all technologies** ignores that sectors and geographies move at different speeds.

## Applying it to your case
- [ ] Where is your trend on the Hype Cycle **today**? Use the most recent **sector-specific** Gartner Hype Cycle, not just the general emerging-tech one.
- [ ] What is the **time-to-plateau** estimate, and does it fit your company's decision horizon?
- [ ] If the trend is at or near the peak, what is your evidence that the benefit is real rather than narrative?
- [ ] Cross-reference against Rogers: hype position vs actual adoption. **The gap is where your argument lives.**
