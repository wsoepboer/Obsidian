## What it is
Everett Rogers, sociologist, first published 1962 (5th edition 2003). The theory explains **how, why, and at what rate** a new idea or technology spreads through a social system.

Core insight: adoption is not a single event but a **social process over time**. People adopt at different rates, and who adopts when is predictable enough to plan around.

---

## The five adopter categories
Adoption over time follows a roughly normal distribution, so cumulative adoption traces an **S-curve**.

| Category | % of population | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Innovators** | 2.5% | Venturesome, risk-tolerant, financial cushion to absorb failure, technical grasp. Often socially peripheral — the group doesn't take cues from them. |
| **Early adopters** | 13.5% | **Opinion leaders.** Respected and well integrated. The critical group — their adoption legitimises the innovation for everyone after. |
| **Early majority** | 34% | Deliberate, adopt just before the average. Rarely hold leadership positions. Need to see it work first. |
| **Late majority** | 34% | Sceptical. Adopt out of economic necessity or peer pressure, not enthusiasm. |
| **Laggards** | 16% | Traditional, reference point is the past. Often isolated in the network. Adopt when the alternative has disappeared. |

> [!tip] Where the leverage is
> Innovators adopt regardless and influence nobody; the majorities wait for social proof. Winning **early adopters** is what moves the curve.

## The five attributes that determine adoption rate
The part most useful for the trend analysis — criteria to argue **why** your trend will or won't spread. The kick-off deck reproduces this table almost exactly, under *"When are innovations successful? When they are broadly adopted."*

| Attribute | Effect | Question, as the deck puts it |
|---|---|---|
| **Relative advantage** | ↑ | What problem does it solve? What is the added value — uniqueness of need, value and financial return? |
| **Compatibility** | ↑ | How does it work with other elements? Compliance with existing values, past experiences and the needs of potential adopters. |
| **Complexity** | **↓** | How difficult or easy is it to use? The extent to which it is perceived as hard to understand and use. |
| **Trialability** | ↑ | The extent to which it can be experimented with. |
| **Observability** | ↑ | Are the results of the innovation visible to others? |

![[slide-42.jpg]]

All five are about **perception**, not objective fact. A technically superior innovation with poor perceived relative advantage still fails.

## The innovation-decision process
1. **Knowledge** — becomes aware it exists
2. **Persuasion** — forms an attitude toward it
3. **Decision** — commits to adopt or reject
4. **Implementation** — puts it into use
5. **Confirmation** — seeks reinforcement; may still reverse

Useful for Part 2: your implementation plan should say **which stage** your target audience is in, because the intervention differs. Knowledge needs information; Persuasion needs social proof; Implementation needs training and support.

---

## Extension — Moore's Chasm
Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (1991): there is a **gap between early adopters and the early majority** that many innovations never cross. Early adopters want a **change agent** and tolerate roughness; the early majority wants a **productivity improvement** and demands a complete, reliable, supported product with references from people like them.

Not part of Rogers' original model, but naming it shows range.

## Criticisms
Worth one line in written work — Boris rewards argument over concept.

- **Pro-innovation bias** — assumes innovations should be adopted and treats rejection as a failure to explain rather than a rational choice.
- **Individual-blame bias** — locates non-adoption in the individual rather than in a badly designed innovation or an unequal system.
- **Recall problem** — adoption timing is usually self-reported from memory.
- **Equality** — diffusion often widens gaps, because those with resources adopt first and compound the advantage.

- [ ] Where is your trend on the curve **right now**, in your specific industry? Adoption differs by sector — AI is early majority in marketing, innovator stage in agriculture.
