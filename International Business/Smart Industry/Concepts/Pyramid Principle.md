# Pyramid Principle

**Barbara Minto, 1987**, written while she was a consultant at McKinsey. Consultants are hired to investigate and analyse strategic problems and recommend as neutral outsiders, and they constantly have to persuade people to change — so they need well-structured, logical arguments that together tell a convincing story. Minto's aim was to let her colleagues use lots of information in a story, message or presentation **without losing the detail or the power of the message**. → [[Additional Information Pyramid Principle|source text]]

## The set-up: SCQA
- **Situation** — description of the topic that resonates with the reader.
- **Complication / problem** — what you are solving, the tension the company feels.
- **Question** — what emerges from the complication. To the point, brief, unambiguous.
- **Advice / recommendation** — the *answer* to that question.

## The pyramid, three levels
1. **Start with the answer** — your conclusion, advice or recommendation opens the communication.
2. **Support it with arguments** — **3 to max 5** important points that together prove the answer.
3. **Support each argument with data and facts** — the evidence layer, sourced.

## Worked example from the deck
> *Situation:* offline fashion retailer MKFash grew revenue consistently through high-street locations.
> *Complication:* revenue growth stalled over two years — Corona, and customers moving online.
> *Question:* how can MKFash recapture customers and grow revenue again?
> *Recommendation:* open an online sales channel and adopt an omni-channel strategy that uses its profitable physical stores.
>
> **Argument 1 — open an online channel:** customers are increasingly online (source) · online will keep growing (source) · the investment is not prohibitive (source).
> **Argument 2 — adopt omni-channel:** customers like the choice of off- and online (source) · it is a competitive advantage over web-only shops (source) · a well-designed omni-channel strategy generates efficiencies and savings (source).

## Why it is in week 3
Your assessment is a short pitch with a long Q&A. Answer-first structure is what survives that format, and the defense criterion rewards specific, well-structured, sourced answers.
