# Trend Classification

Sorting a phenomenon into the right category before you build an argument on it. Week 3 grades this explicitly, **with models and sources**.

## The lecturer's distinction
- **Hype** — becomes very popular very quickly through excitement, marketing or buzz, and fades just as fast. Short-lived, media- and influencer-driven, lots of talk without lasting change.
- **Trend** — develops gradually and lasts longer. Reflects a **deeper shift** in behaviour, preferences, technology, culture or society. Influences how people think and act over time, with a more permanent impact.

Short form: *hype = fast rise, loud, brief. Trend = slower, broader adoption, long-term influence.*

## A working ladder
- **Hype / fad** — steep rise, no structural cause, collapses.
- **Micro trend** — visible in a niche or single segment.
- **Trend** — 5-10 years, changes behaviour across an industry. **This is the level the assignment wants.**
- **Megatrend** — 10-30 years, cross-industry and global (ageing, urbanisation, climate).
- **Adopted norm** — diffusion complete; context, not a trend.

## The two tests to apply
1. **[[Diffusion of Innovation]]** — has the majority already adopted it? If yes, it is context.
2. **[[Gartner Hype Cycle]]** — where do expectations sit relative to reality? Peak with no adoption means hype.

Class examples on the hype side: **NFTs**, consumer **3D printing**. Case used for a real trend: the **self-checkout**, launched in the Netherlands in 2005 and now ordinary.
