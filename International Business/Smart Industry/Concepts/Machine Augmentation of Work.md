# Machine Augmentation of Work

Technology **raising the output of a worker** rather than replacing the worker. The distinction that runs through the whole course:

- **Automation** — the machine performs the task; the job disappears or is redefined.
- **Augmentation** — the machine performs part of the task; the human keeps judgement, context and accountability.

Most real cases are **task-level, not job-level**: jobs are bundles of tasks, and technology takes some of them. Which is why both "X% of jobs will be lost" headlines and blanket reassurance are usually wrong.

PwC's ladder makes the same point across four modes, from human-in-the-loop to none: **automated → assisted → augmented → autonomous intelligence**.

## The numbers to argue with
From WEF *Future of Jobs 2025*: churn of **22% of today's jobs** by 2030, **+170 million created**, **-92 million displaced**, net **+78 million**. **39%** of existing skill sets transformed or outdated. **59 of every 100 workers need training by 2030**, and **11 of them will not get it**. **40%** of employers expect to cut workforce where AI can automate tasks, while **half** plan to re-orient the business around AI.
