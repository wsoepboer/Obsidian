# Kondratieff Waves

Long economic cycles of roughly **40-60 years**, each opened by a cluster of general-purpose technologies and closed once that cluster is fully diffused. Named after Nikolai Kondratieff. Conventional sequence: **1** steam and textiles · **2** rail and steel · **3** electricity and chemicals · **4** oil, cars and mass production · **5** information and telecommunications · **6** contested.

## The course reading: Grinin & Grinin
The set text is Grinin, L. & Grinin, A., *The Sixth Kondratieff Wave and the Cybernetic Revolution* (`Files/`). Their argument:

- History divides into **production principles**, changed by **production revolutions**, each with three phases: an **initial innovative** phase, a long **modernization** phase (diffusion, synthesis, improvement), and a **final innovative** phase where the technologies mature.
- The **Cybernetic Revolution started in the 1950s** with its scientific and information phase.
- The **sixth K-wave, in the 2030s-2040s**, will merge with the Cybernetic Revolution's final phase — the **phase of self-regulating systems**.
- That phase is driven by a breakthrough in **medical technologies** which fuse into a single complex: **MBNRIC — med-bio-nano-robo-info-cognitive technologies**.

## Why it opens the course
It reframes today's disruption as **one instance of a repeating pattern**: you can ask what firms did last time instead of treating AI as unprecedented. The lecturer's slide question is the point of the whole exercise — *what does this mean for management, thinking HR?*

**Watch out:** wave dating is disputed and predictive power is weak. Use it as a framing device, not as evidence.
