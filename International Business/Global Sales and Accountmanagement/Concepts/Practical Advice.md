# Practical Advice

The first of the six **Excellent** markers (*"++ Practical advice"*), and a sentence the assessment matrix adds to the learning outcome that the Brightspace *Welcome* text leaves out:

> The student is able to provide **practical advice applicable to an organisation**.

So it is both a graded criterion and the route above *Good*. It is also the single most common failure mode in a report like this: a well-researched analysis that never quite tells the company what to do.

What separates advice from analysis:

| Analysis | Advice |
|---|---|
| "The market is growing and competition is fragmented" | "Enter via a distributor in Q2; here are three candidates and the terms to seek" |
| "Certification requirements differ" | "Budget €X and four months for approval; start with the two SKUs that already meet the standard" |
| "Trade fairs are important in this sector" | "Exhibit at *named fair*, month, ~€Y, target eight distributor meetings" |

The tests: is it **specific** (named, costed, sequenced), is it **feasible** for a company of Altrex's size, is it **owned** by someone, and could the reader act on it on Monday.

Practical advice is also what makes the other Excellent markers pay off — a detailed legal or logistical analysis that ends in an instruction reads as expertise; one that ends in an observation reads as homework.
