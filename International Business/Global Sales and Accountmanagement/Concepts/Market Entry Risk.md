# Market Entry Risk

The rubric names **nine** risk categories, and naming them is the requirement:

> The risks associated with entering a new market (**financial, legal, political, logistical, market, cultural, operational, reputational and corruption-related** risks) are identified and discussed from at least **legal, financial, marketing, cultural and logistical** perspectives.

| Risk | For an exporter of safety-critical equipment |
|---|---|
| **Financial** | Non-payment, credit exposure, currency, margin erosion |
| **Legal** | Certification failure, product liability, contract enforceability, agency law |
| **Political** | Instability, policy change, sanctions, procurement bias |
| **Logistical** | Freight cost and delay, customs, damage, spare-parts supply |
| **Market** | Demand misjudged, entrenched competitors, price pressure |
| **Cultural** | Misread buying process, relationship failure, communication breakdown |
| **Operational** | Capacity, quality across distance, after-sales and service |
| **Reputational** | A safety incident, a poor distributor acting in your name |
| **Corruption** | Facilitation demands, intermediary conduct → [[Corruption Risk]] |

> [!important] Nine categories × five perspectives is a grid, not an essay
> The structure is explicit, so use it: table the nine, and for each say what could happen, how likely, what it would cost, and what mitigates it. Then attach the mitigations to the [[Market Entry Strategy]] — an entry mode is partly a risk decision, and saying so connects two graded criteria.
