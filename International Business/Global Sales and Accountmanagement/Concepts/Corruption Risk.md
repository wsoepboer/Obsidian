# Corruption Risk

The ninth of the rubric's named risk categories, and the one it singles out by name rather than folding into legal or political risk — *"corruption-related risks"*.

It matters in an export context because the exposure is often **not your own conduct**. Facilitation payments demanded at customs, an agent or distributor paying on your behalf, or a public-tender process that expects them — all of it can attach to the exporting company through anti-bribery law that applies extraterritorially, and through the acts of intermediaries.

Handled properly in a report, it produces three concrete things: a **country-level assessment** of the exposure (perception indices are a legitimate, citable starting point → [[Traceable Sources]]), a **channel implication** — the more you sell through intermediaries you do not control, the more of this risk you inherit, which feeds [[Market Entry Strategy]] — and a **mitigation**: due diligence on partners, anti-corruption clauses and audit rights in contracts, refusal policies, and training.

This is also where *"responsible commercial decisions"* in the learning outcome stops being an adjective → [[Responsible Commercial Decisions]].
