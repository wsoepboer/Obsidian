# Customer Retention and Development

The other half of the rubric's requirement: *"how to **retain and further develop** customer relationships"* — the learning outcome's **maintain and expand**.

The distinction is worth keeping sharp, because they need different actions:

- **Retention** — keeping the customer. Reliability of delivery, after-sales and spare parts, technical support, responsiveness to problems, contract and pricing continuity. Most churn in industrial supply is a service failure, not a price decision.
- **Development** — growing the customer. More of the range, more sites or subsidiaries, longer contracts, and moving from supplier to specified standard.

Why it is graded alongside acquisition: in export markets, acquisition is expensive and slow, so the economics of an entry usually depend on retention and expansion rather than on new logos. A recommendation that plans only how to win customers has not made the market pay for itself.

Two things make the section credible: **named mechanisms** (service-level commitments, account reviews, training for the customer's staff, stock availability) and **measurement** — retention rate, revenue per account, share of the customer's spend → [[Data-Driven Commercial Decisions]].
