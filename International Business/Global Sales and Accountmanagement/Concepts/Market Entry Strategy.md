# Market Entry Strategy

How a company enters a market: exporting directly, via agents or distributors, licensing, a joint venture, a sales subsidiary, or acquisition. The choice trades **control** against **commitment and risk** — an agent is cheap, fast and gives you little control over how your product is sold; a subsidiary gives control at the cost of capital and exposure.

The rubric's requirement here contains the sharpest instruction in the whole document:

> The student substantiates their recommendation on the market-entry strategy for a selected country and **explains why certain entry strategies are not recommended**.

> [!important] The rejected options are graded
> Most students recommend one mode and defend it. The rubric asks for the alternatives to be **named and ruled out with reasons** — which is the difference between a choice and a preference. Do it as a short comparison: mode, control, investment, speed, risk, why not.

For Altrex — a bulky, safety-certified physical product with an existing dealer presence in many markets — the realistic candidates are distributor, agent and sales subsidiary, and the deciding factors will be certification liability, logistics cost and how technical the sale is.
