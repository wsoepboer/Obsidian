# Traceable Sources

The rubric's phrase, used at both grade levels: the country analysis must use **"current, reliable and traceable sources."** Three tests, and each rules out a common shortcut.

- **Current** — a market figure from five years ago describes a market that no longer exists. Tariffs, standards and demand all move.
- **Reliable** — statistical agencies, government trade bodies, industry associations, standards organisations, established market research, peer-reviewed work. Not a content-marketing blog citing an unnamed report.
- **Traceable** — the reader can follow the citation to the original. A number quoted from a source that itself does not cite anything is not traceable, however authoritative it sounds.

> [!danger] Referencing is a mandatory criterion
> *"Meets the source-referencing standard at NLQF level"* is one of the six pass/fail gates — miss it and the maximum grade is a **near pass (5)**, no matter how good the advice is. This is the cheapest criterion in the matrix to satisfy and an expensive one to fail.

Two working habits: cite to the **primary** source rather than to whoever quoted it, and record the source as you collect the number rather than reconstructing citations at the end. Note also that GenAI output is not a source — the tool must be declared and the fact still needs a real citation → [[AI Policy - IB Programme]].
