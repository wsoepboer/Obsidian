# Influencer Marketing

Fifth and last of the named [[Account Strategy]] elements, and the one that looks out of place in an industrial-equipment assignment — which is exactly why it is worth thinking about properly rather than importing a consumer playbook.

In a B2B and trade context the analogue of an influencer is a **credible practitioner or specifier**: trade professionals with a following, safety trainers and inspectors, industry associations, standards bodies, testing institutes, trade media and reviewers, and the tradespeople whose tool choices others copy.

What they supply is not reach but **endorsement under risk**. For safety-critical equipment, a recommendation from someone who is personally liable for using it carries more weight than any campaign, and it travels through exactly the networks a new entrant lacks.

Practical treatment: identify who those voices are **in the assigned countries** (they are almost always local), say what the relationship would be — product placement, demonstrations, training partnerships, association membership — and address the compliance and reputational side, since an endorsement of an uncertified use is a liability → [[Market Entry Risk]].
