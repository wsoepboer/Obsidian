# Country Selection

Which countries the analysis covers — and in this assignment, mostly not your decision. The brief:

> As Altrex already has a strong presence in many markets, **you will be assigned specific countries** to focus on.

So the selection work is not choosing markets but **understanding the ones you were given**, and the recommendation must be *"specifically tailored to the country or countries assigned to you."* Generic country content is the most visible way to lose marks here.

The rubric adds one requirement of its own: **at least one EU and one non-EU country**. That pairing is deliberate — it forces the contrast that makes the six perspectives bite:

| | EU country | Non-EU country |
|---|---|---|
| Tariffs and customs | Internal market | Duties, clearance, origin rules |
| Certification | Largely harmonised | Separate approval regime |
| Currency | Often none | Usually exposure |
| Legal recourse | Common framework | Jurisdiction-specific |

If the assigned countries do not already satisfy the EU / non-EU floor, that is a question for the lecturer before you start writing.

> [!tip] The screening theory still earns marks
> Not choosing the countries does not make [[Country Selection Model|the selection model]] irrelevant — it changes what you use it for. Its criteria are how you evidence *why* an assigned market is attractive and where it is hard, how you rank your assigned countries against each other to pick the one the entry strategy is built around, and where half the risk list comes from.
