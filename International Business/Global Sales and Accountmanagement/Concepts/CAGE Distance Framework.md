# CAGE Distance Framework

Ghemawat's answer to the claim that the world is flat: **distance still matters**, and it is four things at once, not one. Two countries can be neighbours geographically and far apart in every other sense.

| Distance | What it covers | Where it bites |
|---|---|---|
| **C — Cultural** | Language, values, norms, buying and negotiation behaviour, trust, attitudes to risk and hierarchy | Sales approach, relationship-building, product meaning → [[Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions]] |
| **A — Administrative / political** | Trade blocs, tariffs, standards regimes, currency, legal system, corruption, political relations, colonial and treaty ties | Certification, contracts, duties, recourse → [[Trade Barriers and Tariffs]], [[Corruption Risk]] |
| **G — Geographic** | Physical distance, borders, sea access, transport infrastructure, time zones, climate | Freight cost and lead time — decisive for bulky, low-value-density goods → [[Cross-Border Logistics]], [[Transportation Modes]] |
| **E — Economic** | Income level, cost structures, purchasing power, quality of infrastructure and institutions, availability of skilled labour and channels | Pricing, willingness to pay, channel quality, whether the product is even affordable |

## Two properties that make it more than a checklist

**Distance is bilateral, not a country score.** It is measured *from your home base*, so the same market is near for one exporter and far for another. A country profile is a property of the country; CAGE distance is a property of the **pair**.

**Distance is asymmetric in effect.** Which distance hurts depends on the product. Cultural distance dominates for products carrying meaning or requiring consultative selling; geographic distance dominates for heavy, bulky or low-value-per-kilo goods; administrative distance dominates for regulated and safety-certified products. Name the one that dominates *your* product instead of writing four equal paragraphs.

> [!tip] It maps almost one-to-one onto the rubric
> C → cultural · A → legal · G → logistical · E → economic and financial. Four of [[The Six Perspectives]] arrive already structured, which makes CAGE the cheapest way to keep the country analysis from becoming a list of unrelated facts. What it does not cover is **marketing** — positioning, channel and promotion still need their own treatment.

## Use in selection

CAGE belongs in the **fine selection** stage of the [[Country Selection Model]], as a criterion, and in the risk discussion as a source of failure modes. It is also the measurable version of *psychic distance*, the perceptual construct behind the [[Uppsala Model]] — Uppsala says firms move to near markets first; CAGE says what "near" means and lets you check whether the near market is actually the attractive one.

Where distance is large but the market is attractive, the standard response is not to abandon it but to **bridge** it: a local distributor or agent for cultural and administrative distance, regional warehousing for geographic distance, a repositioned or simplified offer for economic distance → [[Market Entry Strategy]].
