## The learning outcome assessed
> The student analyses international market opportunities and develops a well-substantiated export and account strategy for an organisation, integrating cultural, legal, logistical and financial considerations. The student is able to provide practical advice applicable to an organisation. The student is able to provide strategic advice on building, maintaining and expanding international customer relationships and to make responsible commercial decisions based on market data, risk analysis and organisational objectives.

## Pass mark
> All mandatory criteria must be met; otherwise, the highest possible grade is a near pass (5).
>
> To pass the assessment unit, each part of the learning outcome must receive a grade of at least satisfactory (6).
>
> If a learning outcome is divided into parts, each part carries equal weight unless a specific weighting is assigned to each part.
>
> In accordance with the assessment plan, the final result is recorded as either a numerical grade or a qualitative judgement.
>
> Example of calculating the final grade when the learning outcome is divided into three parts: (grade for learning outcome part 1 + grade for learning outcome part 2 + grade for learning outcome part 3) / 3 = final grade. Example of calculating the final grade when the learning outcome is divided into three parts and part 1 carries double weight: ((grade for learning outcome part 1 x 2) + grade for learning outcome part 2 + grade for learning outcome part 3) / 4 = final grade.

## The criterion
> The student analyses international market opportunities and develops a well-substantiated export and account strategy for an organisation, integrating cultural, legal, logistical and financial considerations. The student is able to provide practical advice applicable to an organisation.
>
> The student is able to provide strategic advice on building, maintaining and expanding international customer relationships and to make responsible commercial decisions based on market data, risk analysis and organisational objectives.

### Satisfactory — *the learning outcome has largely been achieved*
The document introduces this level with the question *"When does the student's performance largely meet the learning outcome?"*

> The student has analysed and mapped the export opportunities for **at least one EU country and one non-EU country**, using **current, reliable and traceable sources**. The student has substantiated the recommendation from **economic, financial, cultural, marketing, logistical and legal** perspectives.
>
> Furthermore, the student has provided a well-supported recommendation on **account management**, including advice on the **composition of the sales team, communication channels, participation in relevant trade fairs, online strategies and influencer marketing**, while taking cultural, legal, logistical and financial considerations into account.
>
> The risks associated with entering a new market (**financial, legal, political, logistical, market, cultural, operational, reputational and corruption-related risks**) are identified and discussed from at least **legal, financial, marketing, cultural and logistical** perspectives.
>
> The student substantiates their recommendation on the **market-entry strategy** for a selected country and **explains why certain entry strategies are not recommended**. In addition, the student advises on how to **acquire** international customers in the chosen target markets and how to **retain and further develop** customer relationships. Finally, the student demonstrates the use of **relevant data** to support decision-making and shows that these decisions are **aligned with the organisation's objectives**.

### Good — *the learning outcome has fully been achieved*
> The student has **thoroughly** analysed and mapped the export opportunities for at least one EU country and one non-EU country, using current, reliable and traceable sources.
>
> The student provides a **detailed and well-substantiated** recommendation from economic, financial, cultural, marketing, logistical and legal perspectives.
>
> The student has also provided a **detailed and well-supported** account management recommendation, including advice on the composition of the sales team, communication channels, participation in relevant trade fairs, online strategies and influencer marketing. In doing so, the student has taken cultural, legal, logistical and financial considerations into account.
>
> Furthermore, the student substantiates their recommendation on the market-entry strategy for a selected country and explains why certain entry strategies are not recommended.
>
> The student also advises on how to acquire international customers in the chosen target markets and how to retain and further develop customer relationships.
>
> The risks associated with entering a new market—including financial, legal, political, logistical, market, cultural, operational, reputational and corruption-related risks—are identified and discussed from at least legal, financial, marketing, cultural and logistical perspectives.
>
> Finally, the student demonstrates the use of relevant data to support decision-making and shows that these decisions align with the organisation's objectives.

### Excellent — *exceeds elements of the learning outcome*
> ++ Practical advice
> ++ Detailed legal and financial implications
> ++ Logistical impact analyses
> ++ Top customer segment
> ++ Trade fairs
> ++ Data analysis

> [!important] Satisfactory and Good differ by four words
> Put the two descriptors side by side and the content is identical. The only changes are **"thoroughly"** analysed, a **"detailed and well-substantiated"** export recommendation, and a **"detailed"** account recommendation.
>
> That is unusually clear guidance: *Good* is the same report done properly. Named sources with figures, specific channels and named trade fairs, entry modes actually costed, and the rejected options argued rather than mentioned. There is no extra section to add.

## General notes on assessment (verbatim)
> \* The distinction between largely and fully is not quantitative (part or all of the learning outcome), but qualitative (the level of proficiency, depth, scope, complexity of the work, etc.).
>
> \*\* An assessment is always holistic in nature. This means that examples and feedback provided to illustrate an assessment apply to the work as a whole. For reassessment, you must therefore review your entire body of work in light of the feedback and revise it where necessary, rather than addressing only the specific example mentioned.

## Purpose (verbatim)
> The purpose of the assessment matrix is to assess the learning outcomes in a valid, reliable and transparent manner. It also has a formative function: it helps students understand what they are working towards. When a product or performance is assessed using the matrix, students gain insight into their capabilities, which can support their learning. Lecturers also receive information that enables them to adjust their teaching and/or guidance.
>
> The following definitions of 'satisfactory' and 'good' are used in the assessment matrices:
>
> **Satisfactory:** the student's performance largely meets the description of the learning outcome.
> **Good:** the student's performance fully meets the description of the learning outcome.
