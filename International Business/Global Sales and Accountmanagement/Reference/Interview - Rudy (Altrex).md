## Company profile
- **Altrex** — manufacturer of stairs, ladders and scaffolding equipment, headquartered in **Zwolle**, operating internationally
- Product range: **stepladders, ladders, mobile towers, suspended platforms**
- **Current project focus: aluminium mobile towers**
- Offices: **Netherlands, Belgium, and Spain (Catalonia specifically)**

## Customer segments
Five named segments:
- Retail DIY
- Retail professional
- Online
- Rental
- End user (companies that need scaffolding for their own use, not for resale)

**For new-country expansion, the focus is retail professional and online** — specifically **pure players / specialised** retailers, plus rental. Retail DIY is explicitly **not** the focus for new markets.

## Market entry precedent — Germany
Altrex entered Germany using a **German partner on a private-label basis** — Altrex products sold under the partner's own brand. This is a live example of an entry mode already in use, not a textbook option: worth using directly in the market-entry strategy section (Phase 5 of [[Project Plan - Altrex]]) rather than only citing Uppsala/Stages of Internationalisation in the abstract.

## Markets under consideration (Altrex's own list, per Rudy)
Missing/target markets named: **Africa, Germany*, Eastern Europe, South America**. **Singapore** was also raised — not as a target market itself, but as a **hub to gain presence in Asia**.

*(Germany is listed as "missing" here despite the private-label entry above — read as either an outdated note or a market Altrex wants to deepen beyond the current partner arrangement. Worth clarifying with Rudy or Chiel rather than assuming.)*

This can mean either a wholly new country, or a new market segment within a country Altrex is already in.

## Strategic context — why these markets, why now
- **The DIY market is expected to shrink over the next ~50 years**, driven by generational change in how younger people approach home improvement. This is the stated reason retail DIY is de-prioritised for new-country expansion in favour of retail professional and online.
- **Structural growth drivers** cited: construction, infrastructure, disaster relief, demographic change, and changing weather patterns altering construction requirements.
- **End-customer segment** (companies needing scaffolding for their own operations, not resale) named as a distinct opportunity, with specific verticals:
  - Nuclear power reactor construction/maintenance
  - Aircraft maintenance (worth tracking where air travel is growing)
  - Ship maintenance and other mobility infrastructure
  - Archaeology, mining, restoration
  - Post-disaster and emergency response — emergency bridges, search and rescue
  - Vineyards and similar ground-level agriculture

These verticals are candidate **top customer segments** (an Excellent marker in the rubric) for both Denmark and Sub-Saharan Africa — worth screening each vertical against what actually exists in each assigned market before committing.

## Product strategy and compliance (Q&A)

**Q: How have recent compliance changes and standards impacted the R&D and sales process?**
A: Compliance requirements differ by country and change over time. In practice, this surfaces in sales meetings: a product version one country will no longer accept may still be sellable in another country, sometimes at a discount to move the older-compliance stock.

**Q: How do you manage product innovation against existing stock — build for worldwide compliance coverage, or specialise per country?**
A: Altrex's approach is **mass production focused on covering as much regulatory ground as possible in one product**, rather than a bespoke product per country. Rudy described the *ideal* sequence as: focus on one country, develop specifically for its requirements, then take that product to market — but current practice leans toward the broad-coverage approach for scale reasons.

## MOQ (minimum order quantity) policy
- **Private-label arrangements typically carry an MOQ.**
- **For new customers or new countries, Altrex sometimes waives the MOQ** to establish the relationship first — but they also use MOQ-based deals. Read as: MOQ is a lever they flex depending on how established the relationship is, not a fixed rule. Worth asking Rudy directly which approach fits a first entry into Denmark or Sub-Saharan Africa.
