# Ch5 — Internationalisation

**Lectures · Organisation, Strategy & HRM** — [[00 Organisational Strategy and HR MOC|↩ Lectures MOC]] · [[Welcome|Course Home]]
**Prev:** [[03 Strategic Management]] · **Next:** [[06 The Future of Work]]


## Overview
Why and how organisations cross borders.

## Internationalisation vs globalisation
[[Internationalisation vs Globalisation]]: internationalisation rose rapidly **after WWII** and again with the **internet**; **outsourcing** moves activities to low-income countries. **Globalisation** is broader — worldwide integration of countries and continents, not just products.

## Economic integration & the EU
[[Forms of Economic Integration]] (increasing depth): free-trade zone → customs union → common market → economic & monetary union → full political & economic union. The **EU** is a common market; 19 states share the euro. Key EU institutions: Commission, European Council, Council of the EU, Parliament, Court of Justice.

## International institutions
[[International Institutions]]: UN, World Bank, IMF, WTO, OECD, WEF, BRICS (NDB & CRA), OPEC.

## International management theories
[[Theories of Internationalisation]] group into **trade**, **static** and **process** theories. Process theories include the [[Uppsala Model]] (gradual stages) and [[Born Globals]] (international from day one — Booking.com, Uber, Alibaba). See the [[Stages of Internationalisation]] (export → licence → alliance → joint venture → merger/takeover → greenfield).

## Outsourcing & offshoring
[[Outsourcing & Offshoring]]: motives are the **4 Cs** (costs, capital, capability, capacity). Offshoring = moving activities **across borders**. Social criticism concerns employment and working conditions (Fairtrade, textile agreements).

## Self-test

- Order the five forms of economic integration.
- Contrast the Uppsala model with born globals.
