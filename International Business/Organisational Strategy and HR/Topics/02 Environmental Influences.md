# Ch2 — Environmental Influences

**Lectures · Organisation, Strategy & HRM** — [[00 Organisational Strategy and HR MOC|↩ Lectures MOC]] · [[Welcome|Course Home]]
**Prev:** [[01 Evolution of Organisation & Management]] · **Next:** [[03 Strategic Management]]


## Overview
Organisations are **open systems** shaped from outside. This chapter covers the external parties, the macro-environment, and corporate responsibility.

## External parties
An organisation interacts with [[External Parties (Stakeholders)]]: customers, suppliers, competition, capital providers, employees, special-interest groups, government institutions and media.

## Environmental factors (the macro-environment)
Six factor groups — environmental, technological, demographic, economic, political, societal — analysed with [[DESTEP Analysis]] (the deck's **STIRDEEPER** variant: Society, Technology, Industry, Resources, Demographics, Economics, Environment, Politics, Energy, Religion).

- **Environmental factors** — driven by political choices/societal consensus; e.g. the **2015 Paris Climate Accord** target of limiting global temperature rise to **≤2°C** above pre-industrial levels.
- **Economic factors** — the key indicator is **national income**, which strongly drives spending patterns.
- **Political factors** include the [[Forms of Economic Integration]] between countries (free-trade zone → full union); political power is increasingly shifting beyond national borders (e.g. to the EU internal market).
- **Societal factors** — organisations increasingly build CSR in as a structural, conscious choice that goes beyond legal obligation, adding value for both society and the company.

## Industry analysis
[[Porter's Five Forces]] assesses the competitive pressure in an industry (e.g. airlines vs big pharma).

## Corporate social responsibility
[[Corporate Social Responsibility]] contrasts the **classic** (Anglo-Saxon, US/UK) view — profit is management's only duty, advocated by Nobel laureate **Milton Friedman** (1976) — with the **social-economic** (Rhineland) view common in NL/DE/CH/Scandinavia: contribute positively to society (**people, planet, profit**), adopted in phases from owner-driven to society-wide responsibility. Related: [[Sustainable Development]] (UN SDGs, Paris 2015, Global Compact, ISO 26000) and **social enterprises** — autonomous, financially self-reliant, socially-managed businesses (Tony's Chocolonely, Rituals, Ben & Jerry's); ~**6% of European employment**, growing fast.
