# Ch12 — Organisational Culture & Ethics

**Lectures · Organisation, Strategy & HRM** — [[00 Organisational Strategy and HR MOC|↩ Lectures MOC]] · [[Welcome|Course Home]]
**Prev:** [[07 Human Resource Management]]


## Overview
The shared values that shape behaviour — and the ethics that guide decisions.

## What culture is
[[Organisational Culture]] — the collection of shared views about the work, each other, oneself and the organisation. Management style is the key ingredient of culture.

## Models of culture
- [[Onion Model]] (Hofstede & Hofstede): **symbols → heroes → rituals → values**.
- [[Iceberg Model]] (McClelland): visible behaviour above the surface, hidden drivers below.
- [[Strong Cultures]] (Robbins & Coulter): widely shared, consistent values.

## Typologies
[[Culture Typologies]] overview covers: group-process approach, **Harrison & Handy**, **Sanders & Neuijen**, professional culture, [[Schein's Three-Layer Model]], [[Kets de Vries & Miller Typology]] (five "neurotic" cultures) and **Quinn & Rohrbaugh**.

## National culture
Comparative frameworks: [[Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions]] (6 dimensions), the [[GLOBE Model]] (9 dimensions) and [[Trompenaars' Cultural Dimensions]] (7 dimensions).

## Ethics
- [[Responsibility and Integrity]]: three responsibilities (liability, task, virtue — Van Dalen); integrity as **moral self-determinism**.
- [[Four Ethical Stances]]: utilitarian, individual/liberal, justice, moral.
- [[Social Responsibility Concepts]]: traditional → social-responsibility → empowerment.

## Self-test
- Recall the four layers of the onion model.
- Name three of Hofstede's cultural dimensions and what a high score means.
- Give the four ethical stances with an example of each.
