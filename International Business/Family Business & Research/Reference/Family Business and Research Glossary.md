# Family Business & Research Glossary

Every term the course introduces, one line each. [[00 Family Business and Research MOC|↩ Course MOC]]

## The field
- **[[Family Business]]** — a company in which a family actively influences the future direction of the business; not merely a company a family owns.
- **[[Three-Circle Model]]** — Tagiuri & Davis (1982); every family business is three overlapping systems — family, ownership, business.
- **[[Generational Stages]]** — how far a firm has travelled from its founder: *parent → children → nephews* (controlling owner → sibling partnership → cousin consortium).
- **Family ownership** — one family owns a significant share; the first of the three defining characteristics.
- **Family influence** — family members influence important decisions; the second characteristic.
- **Long-term intention** — the family intends to pass the company to future generations; the third and most decisive characteristic.

## Distinctive strengths
- **[[Long-Term Orientation]]** — thinking in generations rather than quarters.
- **[[Patient Capital]]** — owners willing to wait longer for returns, funding innovation and long-term growth.
- **[[Family Values]]** — trust, responsibility, stewardship, reputation, commitment; they shape decisions about employees, customers and communities.
- **[[Stewardship]]** — acting as guardian of something valuable so it stays healthy for future generations; owner as caretaker.
- **[[Legacy]]** — what future generations receive and remember: wealth, values, reputation, relationships, social impact, entrepreneurial spirit.

## Distinctive challenges
- **[[Succession]]** — transferring leadership, ownership, knowledge, values and relationships to the next generation.
- **[[Nepotism]]** — opportunities given on relationships rather than competence.
- **[[Role Ambiguity]]** — failure to separate family roles from business roles (also *role confusion*).
- **[[Family Conflict]]** — personal disagreements influencing business decisions.
- **[[Founder Dependency]]** — the organisation succeeds because of the founder and is limited by the same factor.
- **Emotional decision-making** — emotions overriding rational business decisions.

## Succession
- **[[Succession Journey]]** — succession as a five-stage process: awareness → preparation → selection → gradual transfer → formal transfer.
- **[[Successor Readiness]]** — how prepared a potential successor is to lead: competence, commitment, credibility, character.
- **[[Family Logic vs Business Logic]]** — families value fairness and harmony, businesses value the right people in the right positions; the tension is a defining feature of family firms.

## Governance
- **[[Governance]]** — the structures, processes and agreements that let people decide and work together; the "rules of the game".
- **[[Corporate Governance]]** — governing the business: boards, advisory councils, supervisory structures; brings outside perspective and curbs emotional decisions.
- **[[Family Governance]]** — governing the family: structured dialogue so the family stays aligned.
- **[[Family Council]]** — a formal forum for ownership, values, succession and long-term ambitions, held outside daily management.
- **[[Family Charter]]** — a document capturing family values, family-employment rules, ownership expectations and conflict procedures.
- **[[Shareholder Agreement]]** — the legally binding agreement between owners; the charter's legal counterpart.
- **[[Ownership Governance and Management]]** — the three-role separation: who owns, who oversees, who runs it day to day.

## Family relationships
- **[[Family System]]** — the family and business as two systems continuously influencing each other; families as *emotional systems*.
- **[[Family Dynamics]]** — the four recurring patterns: loyalty, rivalry, dependency, conflict avoidance, each double-edged.
- **[[Communication in Family Businesses]]** — where expectations go unspoken, assumptions fill the gap.
- **[[Constructive vs Destructive Conflict]]** — constructive conflict attacks ideas and strengthens decisions; destructive conflict attacks persons and erodes trust.

## Professionalisation
- **[[Professionalisation]]** — building the structures, capabilities and practices that let a firm grow without depending on a few individuals; not becoming less entrepreneurial.
- **[[Leadership Development]]** — identifying, preparing and supporting future leaders, family and non-family, on capability rather than entitlement.
- **[[Greiner's Growth Model]]** — growth as alternating evolution and crisis; required for the session 5 deliverable but not taught on Brightspace.

## Continuity
- **[[Future-Proofing]]** — balancing continuity with change so the firm is still successful in twenty years.
- **[[Innovation Across Generations]]** — innovating while preserving core values; too much change threatens identity, too little threatens survival.
- **[[Sustainability in Family Business]]** — beyond the environmental: social responsibility, employee wellbeing, ethical behaviour, community impact, long-term value creation.
