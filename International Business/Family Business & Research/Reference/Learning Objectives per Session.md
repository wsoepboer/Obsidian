# Learning Objectives per Session

The course learning outcome, dissected by Brightspace into objectives per session. [[00 Family Business and Research MOC|↩ Course MOC]] · [[Family Business & Research - Course Info|Course Info]]

## The learning outcome
Verbatim from Brightspace:

> The student analyses and evaluates the major aspects of international organisations, including business environment, strategy, internationalisation, leadership, and organisational culture, with a specific focus on family businesses. They identify key dimensions such as business, family, and ownership systems, and assess their impact on organizational processes, governance, succession, and value creation. By applying theories and models related to family business management, the student conducts research—through desk or field methods, business cases, or real-world situations—to examine challenges and changes in family businesses. They apply creative problem-solving and critical thinking skills to develop detailed, realistic recommendations, and present these solutions effectively as a family business advisor using storytelling techniques.

Brightspace's own note: *"As learning outcomes are written in a broad sense, we have dissected this learning outcome in learning objectives per session."*

> [!tip] Read the outcome backwards
> It ends on **develop recommendations → present as a family business advisor → use storytelling**. Everything before that is input. The deliverable is not a description of a family business; it is **advice, delivered persuasively**. Notice also *"desk **or** field methods"* — both routes are explicitly sanctioned, which matters when you choose a [[Research Pathways|pathway]].

## Session 1 — [[S1 - What Makes Family Businesses Unique]]
- ✓ Define what a family business is
- ✓ Explain the distinguishing characteristics of family businesses
- ✓ Describe the economic and social importance of family firms
- ✓ Understand the relationship between family, ownership and business
- ✓ Apply the Three-Circle Model to analyse a family business
- ✓ Explain the advantages and challenges of family ownership

## Session 2 — [[S2 - Surviving Leadership Transitions]]
- ✓ Explain the succession process in family businesses
- ✓ Identify common succession challenges
- ✓ Assess successor readiness
- ✓ Understand the role of founders during transitions
- ✓ Develop recommendations for successful succession planning

## Session 3 — [[S3 - Organising Ownership and Decision-Making]]
- ✓ Explain the purpose of governance
- ✓ Distinguish between corporate and family governance
- ✓ Understand family charters and shareholder agreements
- ✓ Evaluate governance structures in family firms

## Session 4 — [[S4 - How Family Relationships Influence Success]]
- ✓ Recognize common family dynamics
- ✓ Explain the impact of emotions on decision-making
- ✓ Understand conflict patterns
- ✓ Apply communication tools in family business situations

## Session 5 — [[S5 - Professionalisation and Leadership Development]]
- ✓ Explain what professionalization means in a family business context
- ✓ Distinguish between ownership, governance and management roles
- ✓ Understand leadership development challenges in family firms
- ✓ Evaluate professionalization opportunities
- ✓ Recommend actions that support long-term organizational growth

## Session 6 — [[S6 - Remaining Successful for Future Generations]]
- ✓ Explain why innovation is important for continuity
- ✓ Understand sustainability from a family business perspective
- ✓ Explore the concept of stewardship
- ✓ Evaluate legacy creation strategies
- ✓ Develop recommendations for long-term resilience

## The verb ladder
Worth noticing how the verbs escalate across the six sessions — it tells you what depth is expected when:

| Sessions | Dominant verbs | What is expected |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | *define, explain, describe, understand, apply* | Reproduce and use the models |
| **2–4** | *identify, assess, distinguish, recognise, apply* | Diagnose a real firm |
| **3, 5** | **evaluate** | Judge quality, not just describe |
| **2, 5, 6** | **develop / recommend** | Produce advice |

The last row is what the assessment actually grades — see [[Assessment and Deliverables]].
