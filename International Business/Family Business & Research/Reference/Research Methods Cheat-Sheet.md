# 🔬 Research Methods Cheat-Sheet

Quick reference for the research strand. [[00 Family Business and Research MOC|↩ Course MOC]]

> [!info] Scope of this sheet

The method detail itself comes from Verhoeven and from Rob Erven's statistics workshops; fill the empty sections below as those land.

> [!important] Read the appendix list as a method specification
> "Interview protocols" and "transcripts and coding" are the only method words Brightspace uses anywhere — and they are **qualitative field research**. Whatever pathway you choose, budget for at least one interview round, a transcription workflow and a coding scheme. Meanwhile the statistics workshops point at **quantitative** work on a database, so a **mixed-methods** design is the shape the course is implicitly steering towards.

## Interview work — what the appendices require
- **Interview protocol** — the question list per respondent type, produced *before* the interview, and handed in
- **Transcript** — the interview written out; agree a convention (verbatim vs cleaned) across the group and apply it consistently
- **Coding** — labelling transcript fragments and grouping labels into themes; hand in the coding, not just the conclusions

Practical points worth settling in the group at the start: recording consent and how it is asked, anonymisation of respondents, where the raw recordings live, and who owns the coding scheme so that two members do not code the same interview differently.

## To fill from Verhoeven
The scaffold this sheet is built for, once you work through *Doing Research*:

- **Research design decision tree** — problem statement → research question → sub-questions → method
- **Method by method** — literature review, interviews, surveys, case study, observation: strengths and limits of each
- **Sampling** — types and rules of thumb
- **Validity and reliability** — the checks, and triangulation
- **Analysis** — qualitative coding and thematic analysis; descriptive statistics
- **Output** — conclusions vs recommendations, and why the distinction is graded

## Reporting
The portfolio is a **research report** defended in a presentation with a competency-based interview — see [[Assessment and Deliverables]]. The learning outcome ends on **recommendations presented as a family business advisor using storytelling techniques**, so the report's final section is the one that carries the outcome. Conclusions answer the research question; **recommendations tell the family what to do**. They are not the same section and should not read the same way.
