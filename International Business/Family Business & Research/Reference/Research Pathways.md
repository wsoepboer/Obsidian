## 1 — Own / network family business
Find a family business yourself; **it can be your own family's company**.

- **Requires approval** — talk to the professor (or the person heard in the audio as *"Heal"*, still unidentified) **before assuming it is fine**
- **Minimum size: 25 FTE**, multiple departments, **preferably internationally operating**
- **Must have financial data available** — this links directly to the session 6 valuation deliverable: *no financials, no valuation possible*
- **Group: max 4** (you plus at most three others), and you **choose your own members**
  - The self-selection is deliberate: the information may be sensitive — a relative's company — so you control who sees it

> [!tip] Why the FTE floor exists
> 25 FTE with multiple departments is what makes the course's models applicable at all. A five-person firm has no [[Corporate Governance|board]], no [[Ownership Governance and Management|role separation]] to analyse, and no meaningful [[Greiner's Growth Model|Greiner]] phase. The size rule is a research-feasibility rule, not bureaucracy — though whether it is a **hard cutoff** is still an open question.

Sensitive-information handling here also interacts with [[AI Policy]]: clear your AI use with the company, because the consequences of a confidentiality breach are personal, not only academic.

## 2 — Münster Exchange ✅ chosen
A cooperation between **the professorship of Family Business at Windesheim** and **TAFH Münster GmbH** — the central innovation promotion, service and project development organisation of Münster University of Applied Sciences.

**What it is:**
- **Research topic: succession in family businesses** — the **next-generation perspective versus the investor perspective**
- **You interview people in the Netherlands; German students interview in Germany**; the two sets are compared for commonalities and differences
- **Groups of four:** two participants from Germany, two from the Netherlands
- Brightspace's stated theme is **future-proofing family businesses** → [[Future-Proofing]]
- **Extracurricular:** a possible visit to Münster, and hosting TAFH students in Zwolle

**Why take it:**
- **Particularly interesting if you are considering a master's programme** after IB — higher-level research skills than the standard track
- **Narrower but deeper**

> [!note] Brightspace's wording overstates this
> The tile reads *"We particularly **invite** those students who are considering a master programma after they have graduated."* The intended meaning is that the pathway is **particularly interesting** for those students — a statement of **fit**, not of invitation or priority.
>
> The distinction matters because *"we invite"* implies a route in. There is none: places are **drawn by lottery** (below), so being master's-bound does not improve your odds. Read it as *"this suits you"*, not *"we want you"*.

**Topic coverage — 1, 2 and 6 only, by design:**

> [!important] Münster is a special option, not a reduced version of the standard track
> The three-topic scope is **deliberate**. This is a separate route with its own research question, its own international group structure and its own theme — narrower but deeper, aimed at students heading for a master's. It is not the six-topic programme with three sessions missing, so do not read the gaps as something to make up.

The mapping is coherent: the lecture's stated topic is **succession** (2), and Brightspace's stated theme is **future-proofing** (6), with foundations (1) underpinning both.

**What a lottery means in practice:**

- **There is nothing to prepare and nothing to win on merit.** No pitch, no case for why you should be picked — indicating your interest by the deadline is the whole of your input.
- **Being master's-bound does not help you.** Brightspace's *"we particularly invite…"* is meant as **particularly interesting for** those students — a statement of fit, not a selection criterion or a priority claim. It tells you who the route suits; it does nothing to your odds.
- **The real preparation is the fallback.** Since the outcome is random, the only useful hedge is having **Case Study** ready to accept — see below. Do not build plans that only work if Münster comes through.
- **Underdemand cuts the other way.** If fewer than 8 students apply, **students may be assigned** to fill the remaining places. So the route can be allocated to you as well as withheld — if you feel strongly either way, say so rather than relying on the draw.

> [!question] Confirm the deliverable list for this route

>
> Since Münster is a **special option** covering topics 1, 2 and 6, the appendix list presumably follows the narrower scope — but Brightspace publishes only the standard list, so **get the Münster version in writing** rather than assuming which of the five artefacts apply. Sessions 1, 2 and 6 map to the age-of-company mindmap and the valuation scenario; the governance structure, family-relations mindmap and Greiner belong to the topics this route does not cover.

## 3 — Case study
- Apply the theory to a case study, **groups of 4**
- **Coaches assign the groups** — not self-selected
- **The default option** if you do not choose 1 or 2 by the deadline
- **Covers all six topics**, like the own-business path

Brightspace corroborates that a case study is a full-status route, not a consolation prize: the session 3 deliverable explicitly says *"the governance structure of the company **or case study**"*, and the learning outcome sanctions **"desk or field methods"** and **"business cases or real-world situations"**. It remains the realistic fallback if the Münster place does not come through.

## Choosing — what actually differs
- **Access risk.** Pathway 1 depends on a company saying yes and meeting the 25 FTE bar; pathway 2 depends on being selected; pathway 3 has neither risk.
- **Data.** Pathways 1 and 2 give primary interview data, which is what the appendices expect. A case study limits the field-research half — defensible, but you should say so deliberately.
- **Breadth vs depth.** Pathways 1 and 3 cover six topics; pathway 2 covers three, more deeply, with an international comparison.
- **Signal.** Only pathway 2 carries the master's-track signal — and only pathway 2 can refuse you.
