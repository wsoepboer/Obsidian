## Grading logic — from the L01 lecture
Brightspace says only *"group assignment with individual grading"*. The lecture was much more specific, and the detail matters.

**Grade = group report + group defence** (an interview about the report). The defence checks two things:

1. Do you **understand** what is written?
2. Can you **validate where it came from**?

> [!warning] The report and the defence are graded independently
> **A weak report cannot be offset by a strong defence.**
> - **Report insufficient** → it must be repaired or rewritten, *even if the defence was excellent*.
> - **Report excellent but you cannot explain or source it** → this triggers scrutiny of whether the work is genuinely your own → [[AI Policy]].
>
> So there is no trade-off to play. Both have to stand on their own.

**Individual accountability:** every group member must be able to explain **any** part of the group product. You cannot hide behind a group grade. The lecture was explicit that this is an **accreditation requirement**, not a course-policy choice — which means it is not negotiable and will not be relaxed.

> [!tip] What this implies for how you work
> Splitting the report into four sections and each writing one is the setup that fails this. Whatever the division of labour, everyone needs to have read and be able to source the whole thing before the defence.

## Feedback and finalisation
1. After the presentation, your assessor fills in the assessment form.
2. The assessment sheet is added to Brightspace and the result recorded there.
3. You have **five working days** to request an individual meeting to discuss the feedback.
4. **After five days the result is finalised.**

> [!important] Diarise the five days
> The window is short and it closes automatically. Put a reminder in for the day results appear.

## The portfolio
**Main document:** the **research report**.

**Supportive documents, placed in appendices** — Brightspace's list, verbatim:

- the deliverables from each workshop
- mindmap — age of the company
- governance structure
- mindmap case study
- Greiner's Growth Model
- scenario
- interview protocols
- transcripts and coding

The first line is a heading for the next five: the six workshop deliverables *are* the appendix backbone. The last two lines are the **research-methods** evidence — and they are the only place Brightspace says anything about method. **Interview protocols, transcripts and coding** imply qualitative field research with at least one round of interviews, so plan for it even if you choose a case-study pathway.

## The six workshop deliverables
| Session | Deliverable | Brief (verbatim from Brightspace) |
|---|---|---|
| **[[S1 - What Makes Family Businesses Unique\|1]]** | *(none)* | "The first guest lecture's purpose is to introduce the field of family business. Next week a company visit is organised and the assignment will be explained. Your task for this week is to familiarise yourself with the content of this week's lecture and additional resources provided." |
| **[[S2 - Surviving Leadership Transitions\|2]]** | **Mindmap — age of the company** | "What is the age of the company (parent — children — nephews) · What are the implications of the age of the company for managing the company? · What opportunities and threats are there for the future?" |
| **[[S3 - Organising Ownership and Decision-Making\|3]]** | **Governance structure (visual)** | "You need to construct a map showing the governance structure of the company or case study. In support of the visual you describe the role of all entities, you analyse the governance structure to a determine its quality and to come to a possible advice." |
| **[[S4 - How Family Relationships Influence Success\|4]]** | **Mindmap — case study** | "Find an example in which family relations are at the core of the problem of the company. In this mindmap you outline which elements play(ed) a role in the issue." |
| **[[S5 - Professionalisation and Leadership Development\|5]]** | **[[Greiner's Growth Model]]** | "Construct Greiner's Growth Model and answer the following questions: in what phase is the company? · what are the implications for the next phase?" |
| **[[S6 - Remaining Successful for Future Generations\|6]]** | **Private-equity scenario** | "Assuming a private equity firm is interested in purchasing 20% of the shares of the company: what would be an acceptable offer to obtain a 20% share of the company? · What are the implications of such a transaction for the future of the company? Distinguish between a European and a US private equity firm." |

> [!tip] The pattern in all five artefacts
> Every brief has the same shape: **produce the artefact, then say what it implies**. Deliverables 2, 3, 5 and 6 all end in a forward-looking or advisory question — *implications, opportunities and threats, advice, next phase, implications for the future*. The artefact is the cheap half. The marks are in the second sentence, which matches the learning outcome's insistence on **recommendations** ([[Learning Objectives per Session]]).

## Working practice
- **Keep every workshop artefact.** All six go into the appendices; there is no second chance to reconstruct them.
- **Record every interview.** Stated flatly in the lecture, and it is what makes the required transcripts and coding possible at all.
- **Group work, individual accountability.** Know the whole report, not only your section — see the grading logic above.
- **Log your AI use from day one.** A declaration and available prompts are mandatory → [[AI Policy]].
- **Storytelling is examinable.** The learning outcome names it explicitly. Treat the presentation as part of the deliverable, not a formality.
