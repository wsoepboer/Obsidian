# ⚠️ AI Policy

The highest-stakes section of the course. [[00 Family Business and Research MOC|↩ Course MOC]] · [[Family Business & Research - Course Info|Course Info]]

> [!info] Source
> From the **L01 orientation lecture** ([[2026-08-31 L01 - Course Intro and Learning Paths]]), where it took up more time than any other topic. **Brightspace publishes nothing on AI for this course** — this note is the only record, which is a reason to treat it carefully rather than lightly.

## The two hard requirements
1. **Declare how you used AI.** Every use, not a general disclaimer.
2. **Keep your prompts available on request.** Traceability is the standard — you must be able to show what you asked and what came back.

> [!important] Start the log now, not later
> A prompt log cannot be reconstructed after the fact. Keep a running record from lecture 1 onward: date, tool, prompt, what you did with the output. Retrofitting one at hand-in is exactly the situation that looks like concealment.

## Major fraud — the line you cannot cross
**Misusing AI — inventing or forging interviews, responses, or research data — is automatically classified as "major fraud" (Article 10).**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Leniency** | **None.** No first-offence discretion; once classified as major fraud the examination board has no room to decide otherwise |
| **Consequence** | **Cannot proceed to semester 2** |
| **Knock-on** | Can **block the Year 4 "free space"** |

This is the one part of the course where the downside is not a grade. Note what the offence actually is: **fabricating data**, not using AI. The portfolio requires interview protocols, transcripts and coding ([[Assessment and Deliverables]]) — real interviews, really transcribed. Anything invented in that chain is the fraud case.

## If you use your own or your family's business
Discuss with the company **what AI use is allowed** given sensitive company information. The lecture was explicit that consequences here can be **personal, not just academic** — a commercial confidentiality breach does not stop at a mark. Relevant if you take the own-business route in [[Research Pathways]].

## Guidance on good use
The lecturer's framing:

> Understand the topic yourself first, then use AI as a **sounding board**. **"AI reflects the quality of your thinking"** — so think before you prompt.

Which lines up with how the course is assessed. The defence checks whether you understand what is written and can **validate where it came from** — an excellent report you cannot source triggers scrutiny of whether it is your own work ([[Assessment and Deliverables]]). Work you did not think through is exactly what fails that test, regardless of how it was produced.

## Practical checklist
- [ ] Running prompt log started, from L01 onward
- [ ] AI declaration drafted as you go, not at the end
- [ ] Every interview genuinely conducted, recorded and transcribed
- [ ] If own-business path: AI use cleared with the company
- [ ] Every group member able to explain and source any part of the report
