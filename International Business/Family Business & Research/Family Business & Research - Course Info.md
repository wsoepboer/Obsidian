## Learning outcome
Verbatim from Brightspace:

> The student analyses and evaluates the major aspects of international organisations, including business environment, strategy, internationalisation, leadership, and organisational culture, with a specific focus on family businesses. They identify key dimensions such as business, family, and ownership systems, and assess their impact on organizational processes, governance, succession, and value creation. By applying theories and models related to family business management, the student conducts research—through desk or field methods, business cases, or real-world situations—to examine challenges and changes in family businesses. They apply creative problem-solving and critical thinking skills to develop detailed, realistic recommendations, and present these solutions effectively as a family business advisor using storytelling techniques.

Dissected into per-session objectives: [[Learning Objectives per Session]].

Note the three verbs the outcome ends on — **develop recommendations**, **present as an advisor**, **use storytelling**. The deliverable is not a description of a family business; it is advice, delivered persuasively.

## Organisation and teaching format
Two class types alternate:

- **Guest lectures — bi-weekly.** Margré van Dalfsen-Hetebrij leads the six sessions. She is an expert three times over: writing a PhD on family business, personally involved in a family business, and working as a business consultant.
- **Workshops — the week after each session.** Four hours with your own class lecturer: work on that session's deliverable, hold your coach meeting, ask questions about the assignment.
- **Two extra statistics workshops.** Rob Erven, on using statistics against a database.

The rhythm is *session → workshop → session → workshop*: twelve teaching weeks for six sessions, plus two statistics slots.
