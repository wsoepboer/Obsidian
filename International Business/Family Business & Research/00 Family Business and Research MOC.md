## How the course is built
The six sessions are not six topics. They are one argument, and seeing it makes the whole thing easier to hold:

1. **Session 1 sets up a tension** — family, ownership and business are three systems in one organisation ([[Three-Circle Model]]), and every strength this produces has a matching weakness.
2. **Session 2 puts the tension under maximum load** — [[Succession]], where the family's logic of fairness collides head-on with the business's logic of competence.
3. **Sessions 3 and 4 give the two answers.** [[Governance]] is the *structural* answer; [[Communication in Family Businesses|communication]] and healthy [[Constructive vs Destructive Conflict|conflict]] are the *relational* one. Neither works alone: governance without dialogue is empty, dialogue without structure has nowhere safe to happen.
4. **Session 5 scales it** — [[Professionalisation]] moves the firm from depending on people to depending on systems, without losing the [[Family Values|values]] that made it work.
5. **Session 6 extends it in time** — [[Future-Proofing]], [[Stewardship]], [[Legacy]].

Two habits of thought recur throughout and are worth carrying into the report:

- **Every distinctive feature is double-edged.** Fast decisions / emotional decisions. Strong commitment / [[Nepotism]]. Loyalty / no honest feedback. A founder who makes the firm / a founder who caps it. Advice that only names the upside has missed the course.
- **The recommendation is a position on a spectrum, never an extreme.** Too little structure is inefficient, too much is bureaucratic. Too much change threatens identity, too little threatens survival. Both balances are stated explicitly in the material.
