# Succession Journey

[[Succession]] as a **journey, not an event**. The common assumption is that succession happens when the founder retires; in reality successful succession **begins years before** the actual transfer.

Families who wait until the last moment get unnecessary tension and uncertainty. Families who start early get time to develop talent, discuss expectations and decide thoughtfully.

## The five stages
| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. Awareness** | The family realises leadership cannot stay with the current generation forever. This opens the conversation about the future |
| **2. Preparation** | Potential successors gain experience and leadership skills. Many successful firms encourage them to **work outside the family business first** — it builds confidence, credibility and a broader perspective |
| **3. Selection** | Deciding who is best suited. **The most sensitive phase**: family expectations, personal ambitions and business needs rarely align perfectly |
| **4. Gradual transfer** | Founder and successor work **together** through a transition period — learning, mentoring, knowledge transfer. Not an abrupt change |
| **5. Formal transfer** | Only now does leadership and ownership formally change hands |

> [!important] The lesson
> Succession should be viewed as a **long-term development process** rather than a retirement plan.

## Why working outside first matters
Two distinct benefits, worth separating: the successor **builds capability** they could not build inside the firm, and they **earn credibility** that cannot be inherited — see [[Successor Readiness]].
