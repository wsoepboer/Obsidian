# Nepotism

Family members receiving opportunities **based on relationships rather than competence**. One of the five challenges of family ownership named in [[S1 - What Makes Family Businesses Unique]].

## It is the flip side of a strength
Nepotism and **strong commitment** are the same mechanism seen from two sides. The family involvement that produces unusual dedication also produces appointments that would not survive open recruitment. This is the pattern to notice across the whole course: the distinctive features of family firms are **double-edged, not simply good**.

## What the course offers against it
- [[Successor Readiness]] — competence, commitment, credibility, character instead of birth order
- [[Leadership Development]] — **capability rather than entitlement**, and a pipeline that includes non-family employees
- [[Family Charter]] — written rules on family employment, agreed before a specific person is in the frame
- [[Corporate Governance]] — independent board members who can ask the question the family will not
