# Greiner's Growth Model

**Required for the [[S5 - Professionalisation and Leadership Development]] deliverable.**

> [!warning] Brightspace names this model but never explains it
> The session text does not cover Greiner at all — the deliverable simply says *"Construct Greiner's Growth Model."* Everything below the deliverable brief is **standard external knowledge**, not course material. Expect the real treatment from the guest lecture or the workshop, and **confirm which version is wanted** — the phase count differs between Greiner's 1972 and 1998 versions.

## The deliverable
Brightspace, verbatim:

> Construct Greiner's Growth Model and answer the following questions:
> - in what phase is the company?
> - what are the implications for the next phase?

## The model in outline
Larry Greiner's *Evolution and Revolution as Organizations Grow* (Harvard Business Review, 1972; revised 1998) argues that organisational growth is **not smooth**. Each phase of steady growth (**evolution**) ends in a **crisis** (**revolution**) that can only be resolved by changing the way the organisation is managed. The management practice that produced success in one phase is what **causes** the crisis at the end of it.

| Phase | Growth through | Ends in a crisis of |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Creativity** | **Leadership** |
| 2 | **Direction** | **Autonomy** |
| 3 | **Delegation** | **Control** |
| 4 | **Coordination** | **Red tape** / bureaucracy |
| 5 | **Collaboration** | *(1972: left open · 1998 adds a sixth phase — growth through alliances/extra-organisational solutions)* |

## Why it belongs in this course
The phase-1 **crisis of leadership** is [[Founder Dependency]] under another name: the founder's personal, informal, creativity-driven management stops scaling, and the firm must professionalise or stall. Greiner's phase 2 — growth through **direction** — is the founder-centred → organisation-centred shift of [[Professionalisation]].

Read further along the curve and the rest of the course appears too: the **crisis of control** is why [[Corporate Governance]] becomes necessary, and the **red-tape crisis** is Brightspace's *too much bureaucracy reduces entrepreneurial flexibility*.

> [!tip] For the deliverable
> Do not just place the company on the curve. The marks are in *"implications for the next phase"* — name the **crisis the firm is walking into** and the management change that resolves it. That is your advice.
