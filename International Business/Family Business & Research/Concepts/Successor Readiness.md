# Successor Readiness

The extent to which a potential successor is **prepared to successfully lead the business**.

Historically, leadership passed to the **oldest child**. Today most family businesses recognise that leadership requires more than family ties. The course is explicit: **readiness matters more than birth order**.

## The four qualities
| Quality | The test |
|---|---|
| **Competence** | Do they have the skills and knowledge to lead? Can they make strategic decisions, manage people, navigate challenges? |
| **Commitment** | Leadership requires dedication. **Not every family member wants the responsibility** |
| **Credibility** | Employees, customers and stakeholders must believe they are capable. **Respect cannot be inherited; it must be earned** |
| **Character** | Able to protect [[Family Values|family values]] and traditions while preparing the company for future challenges |

The best successors are therefore **not necessarily the oldest family members**, but those with the strongest combination of capability, commitment and leadership potential.

## The one the family cannot give
Competence can be trained and character can be raised, but **credibility comes from outside the family** — which is why [[Succession Journey|working outside the firm first]] matters, and why [[Nepotism]] is self-defeating even when the appointee is competent.

Scaled up from one named successor to a pipeline, this becomes [[Leadership Development]] — *capability rather than entitlement*.
