# Generational Stages

How far a family business has travelled from its founder, and how fragmented its ownership has therefore become. The course frames it in the [[S2 - Surviving Leadership Transitions]] deliverable as **"the age of the company (parent — children — nephews)"**.

| Stage | Ownership shape | What it feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **Parent** | Founder or controlling owner | One person owns and manages everything. Decisions are fast because there is only one person in charge |
| **Children** | Siblings own together | Ownership splits between people who grew up competing with each other. [[Family Dynamics|Rivalry]] and fairness pressures surface |
| **Nephews** | Cousins | **Twelve cousins in different countries** — some work in the business, some do not; some want to reinvest profits, others want higher dividends |

## Why the stage matters
It predicts almost everything else in the course:

- **[[Governance]]** need rises with each stage — this is the mechanism behind *"governance becomes increasingly important across generations"*
- **[[Family Conflict]]** risk rises as preferences diverge
- **[[Communication in Family Businesses|Communication]]** gets harder as new members enter the system
- **[[Founder Dependency]]** is a *parent*-stage problem; **fragmented ownership** is a *nephews*-stage problem — so identifying the stage tells you which advice is even relevant

> [!note] Terminology
> *Parent — children — nephews* is Brightspace's own wording. The **L01 lecture** described the same requirement as identifying whether the company is **1st, 2nd or 3rd generation** — so the two vocabularies map directly onto each other:
>
> | Brightspace | Lecture | Literature |
> |---|---|---|
> | Parent | 1st generation | Controlling owner |
> | Children | 2nd generation | Sibling partnership |
> | Nephews | 3rd generation | Cousin consortium |
>
> Any of the three is defensible; pick one and use it consistently in the report.

> [!tip] It is the first deliverable
> The L01 lecture put this at the top of the list: *after week 1*, a **mind map** — a literature review of general issues in family business to establish broad context, plus **the generation of the company and its implications**. That is Brightspace's session 2 deliverable, so the two sources agree ([[Assessment and Deliverables]]).
