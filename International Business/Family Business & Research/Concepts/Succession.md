# Succession

The transfer of a family business from one generation to the next — **the most critical moment in the life of a family business**. Many family businesses do not survive beyond the **second or third generation**.

> [!important] Succession is not replacing a leader
> It is transferring **leadership, ownership, knowledge, values and relationships** — five things at once, on different timescales.

Managed well, succession strengthens the future of the business. Managed badly, it threatens **both family harmony and business continuity**.

## Why it is hard
It runs across all three circles of the [[Three-Circle Model]] simultaneously: business, ownership, family.

- **For the founder** — handing over control means letting go of something deeply personal. The recurring fears: *Will they make the right decisions? Will they protect what I have built?*
- **For the successor** — stepping into large shoes: pressure to meet expectations, prove themselves to employees, earn the trust of customers, while also wanting to bring their own ideas and style.

It is therefore **the most emotional and strategically important moment** in the firm's life, not a management transition.

## The four questions every family faces
- Who should become the next leader?
- Should ownership remain within the family?
- How do you prepare the next generation?
- What happens when family members disagree?
