# Founder Dependency

**One of the most common challenges in growing family businesses.** A founder who knows every employee, customer and important decision is an **advantage** in the early years: fast decisions, direct communication, opportunities seized immediately.

As the business grows, the same pattern breaks down:

- Employees depend on the founder for **approvals and guidance**
- **Important knowledge stays concentrated in one person**
- Strategic decisions are **delayed** because everything flows through one individual

> [!important] The paradox
> The organisation becomes successful **because of** the founder, but future growth is limited **by the same factor**.

## The shift
[[Professionalisation]] moves the firm from **founder-centred to organisation-centred**: clearer responsibilities, more distributed decision-making, systems that support sustainable growth.

> [!tip] How to frame it in an advice report
> The goal is **not to reduce the founder's influence**, but to **create a business that can thrive beyond the founder**. Founders hear the first framing as a threat and the second as a legacy — and [[Legacy]] is what they already care about.

## Related
- **[[Family Dynamics]]** lists *dependency* as one of the four dynamics — founder dependency is that dynamic seen structurally
- **[[Succession]]** is much harder in a founder-dependent firm, because what has to transfer is undocumented and lives in one head
- **[[Greiner's Growth Model]]** calls the same wall a **crisis of leadership**
