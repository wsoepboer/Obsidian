# Innovation Across Generations

People associate family businesses with **tradition**. Tradition matters, but successful family businesses are **rarely static**: firms that survive multiple generations have usually **reinvented themselves several times** as products, markets and technologies changed.

> [!important] The defining characteristic of long-lasting family firms
> The ability to **innovate while preserving core values**.

## Innovation is not limited to technology
- New **products**
- New **business models**
- New **markets**
- New **partnerships**
- New **ways of working**

## The two-sided risk
| Too much change | Too little change |
|---|---|
| Threatens **identity** | Threatens **survival** |

> [!tip] Same shape as professionalisation
> Compare [[Professionalisation]]: *too little structure = inefficiency · too much bureaucracy = lost flexibility.* Both land on a two-sided risk rather than a recommendation to maximise one thing. Any advice you write should be **positioned on the spectrum**, not planted at one end.

Why [[Long-Term Orientation]] cuts both ways here: long horizons make patient investment possible, and also make a firm slow to abandon what has always worked.
