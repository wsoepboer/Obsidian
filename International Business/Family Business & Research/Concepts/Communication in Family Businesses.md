# Communication in Family Businesses

Communication matters in every organisation, but it is **especially** important in family businesses because people interact through **multiple roles at the same time** — a person may be sibling, shareholder, manager and future owner all at once ([[Role Ambiguity]], [[Three-Circle Model]]).

## The mechanism
> [!important]
> **When expectations remain unspoken, assumptions fill the gap.** Family members may believe they understand one another when in reality they hold very different views of the future.

The scenario the course uses — three family members, three unspoken assumptions:

- One assumes **leadership** will eventually pass to them
- One believes **ownership** should be divided equally
- One has no interest in joining but wants to remain involved as a **shareholder**

None of it discussed openly. The conflict is already built in.

## What successful family businesses do
- Discuss important topics **before they become urgent**
- Encourage members to express concerns, ask questions and share expectations openly
- Treat communication as **building trust and alignment**, not only as solving problems

It matters most during **change** — succession, rapid growth, ownership transitions — because uncertainty breeds misunderstanding.

> [!note] What good communication does not do
> It **does not eliminate disagreement**. It creates an environment where disagreement can be discussed **constructively and respectfully** → [[Constructive vs Destructive Conflict]].
