# Family Business

A company in which a family **actively influences the future direction of the business** — not merely a company a family happens to own.

Defining it looks simple and is not. Ownership percentage alone fails the test: a founder owning 100% and managing personally, two siblings owning together but employing an external CEO, and a family owning **40% of a listed company** while still controlling major strategic decisions all qualify.

## The three common characteristics
1. **Family ownership** — one family owns a significant share.
2. **Family influence** — family members influence important decisions.
3. **Long-term intention** — the family intends to pass the company to future generations.

Characteristic 3 does most of the work. It is what separates a family *shareholding* from a family *business*, and it is the root of [[Long-Term Orientation]], [[Patient Capital]], [[Stewardship]] and [[Legacy]].

## Prevalence
In many countries **more than 70% of all businesses** are family businesses. Large examples that still carry significant family influence: **BMW · Heineken · Lego Group · Walmart**.
