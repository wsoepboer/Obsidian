# Three-Circle Model

**Tagiuri & Davis (1982).** The founding framework of family business studies and still the most widely used. Every family business is **three overlapping systems**:

| Circle | Who is in it |
|---|---|
| **Family** | People related through family relationships |
| **Ownership** | People who own shares |
| **Business** | People who work in the company |

## Why it matters
In most companies, a business decision affects employees. In a family business the same decision affects **family relationships, ownership rights and business performance simultaneously** — which is precisely what makes family-firm decision-making harder, not merely different.

Any individual sits in one, two or all three circles, and their interests differ accordingly. A family member who works in the business but owns no shares wants a career; a shareholder who does not work there wants dividends; someone in all three wants all of it. Most family-business conflict is legible as a clash between *positions in this diagram* rather than between people.
