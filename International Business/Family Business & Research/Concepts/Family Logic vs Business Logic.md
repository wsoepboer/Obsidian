# Family Logic vs Business Logic

Family businesses operate according to **two different sets of rules at the same time**. Named by the course as **one of the defining characteristics of family businesses**.

| | Families value | Businesses value |
|---|---|---|
| **Principle** | **Fairness** — treat children equally, avoid favouritism | The **right people in the right positions** |
| **Priority** | Harmony, preserving relationships | Competence, experience, performance |

## The worked example
A family with three children. All three own shares, but only **one** has the leadership skills to become CEO.

- **Business logic:** obvious — choose the most capable candidate.
- **Family logic:** the other two siblings may feel excluded, disappointed, unfairly treated.

## How successful firms handle it
Not by picking a side. They **hold both** — honouring family relationships while ensuring business decisions support long-term continuity and success. Finding the balance is rarely easy, but it is often essential for surviving across generations.

In practice the resolution is usually **structural rather than interpersonal**: separate the logics onto different objects. Business logic governs *management* appointments; family logic governs *ownership* and its returns — which is precisely what [[Ownership Governance and Management]] and a [[Family Charter]] are for.
