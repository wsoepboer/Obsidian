# Family Dynamics

Every family develops **patterns of behaviour** over time — some communicate openly, others avoid difficult conversations; some encourage independence, others emphasise loyalty and togetherness. When the family enters business together, **those patterns become visible in how the company operates**.

The four dynamics the course names, each **double-edged**:

| Dynamic | The strength | The failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Loyalty** | Long-term dedication, resilience in difficult periods, willingness to sacrifice for the company's future | Members cannot challenge each other, give honest feedback, or make unpopular decisions when relationships are at stake |
| **Rivalry** | Healthy competition and motivation | Lifelong sibling competition — school, sport, parental attention — carries into the business as tension, conflict and poor decisions |
| **Dependency** | Strong founder leadership | Employees, family and even customers become heavily dependent on one individual, making succession and organisational development much harder → [[Founder Dependency]] |
| **Conflict avoidance** | Preserves short-term harmony | Unresolved issues grow. Problems that could have been handled early **develop into larger conflicts** |

Recognising them matters because they shape how the firm makes decisions, manages transitions and prepares for the future.

> [!tip] Loyalty and conflict avoidance
> These two are the same instinct at different intensities — protecting the relationship at the cost of the conversation. A family that reports unusually high harmony is often displaying the second, not the first.
