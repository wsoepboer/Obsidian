# Long-Term Orientation

Family owners **think in generations rather than quarters**. The question shifts from *"How much profit can we make this year?"* to *"How can we strengthen the business for the next generation?"*

Named by the course as **one of the most distinctive characteristics** of family businesses, and one of the three reasons family firms often survive longer — alongside [[Family Values]] and [[Patient Capital]].

## What it explains
- Why [[Sustainability in Family Business|sustainability]] is an easier sell to a family firm than to a listed one — the time horizons already match
- Why [[Stewardship]] is a natural self-description for family owners
- Why external investors with fixed fund lifetimes create friction (the [[S6 - Remaining Successful for Future Generations]] private-equity scenario)

## The flip side
Long horizons can also mean **too little change**, which threatens survival just as surely as too much change threatens identity — see [[Innovation Across Generations]].
