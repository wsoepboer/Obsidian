## 2. Theories and models — where each one does work

| Theory / model | Source | Used for | Report section |
|---|---|---|---|
| SCOR | Association for Supply Chain Management, 2022 | Structuring the supply chain map and process analysis | Supply chain map; tactical/operational analysis |
| Global value-chain governance | Gereffi, Humphrey & Sturgeon, 2005 | Reading Chiquita's sourcing as a hierarchy/captive hybrid — why concentration exists | Tactical/operational analysis |
| Porter's Five Forces | Porter, 2008 | Competitive and bargaining-power context behind buyer substitution risk | Problem framing; analysis |
| Bullwhip effect | Lee, Padmanabhan & Whang, 1997 | Why a perishable chain with no buffer amplifies a single-origin shock | Analysis |
| STIRDEEPER | Paniagua R., 2024 | Macro/geopolitical scan for the Global Dynamics half | Country analysis (Panama) |
| Resilience — redundancy vs flexibility | Christopher & Peck, 2004; Sheffi & Rice, 2005 | Framing the diversification/relational-governance scenarios | Recommendations; scenario evaluation |
| Perishable inventory / postponement | Nahmias, 1982 | Testing whether green-life extension substitutes for buffer stock | Recommendations (postponement scenario) |
| Change management | Kotter, or ADKAR | People/processes/information as one interconnected implementation plan | Implementation plan |
| Compliance frameworks | CSRD, CSDDD, ILO 87/98, SA8000, GLOBALG.A.P. | Viable/feasible/compliant test on every recommendation; labour section of country analysis | Recommendations; country analysis |
| Ten-pillar diagnostic | [[T1 - Foundation of Supply Chain Management]] | Sanity check that no pillar (sourcing, risk, sustainability, digitalisation, quality, KPIs…) is left unaddressed | Cross-check across whole report |

> [!note] Not yet named in course material, available if useful
> Kraljic matrix, make-or-buy, dual sourcing, nearshoring/reshoring, EOQ, S&OP, Incoterms — listed in [[00 Global Dynamics and Supply Chain Management MOC]] as standard theory the course hasn't required. Use only if a specific recommendation needs one; don't force them in for coverage.

## 3. Document wireframe — report skeleton

Each section below states its purpose, which theory it draws on, and what separates a Pass from a Good per the rubric — so the drafting owner knows the bar, not just the topic.

| # | Section | Purpose | Theory applied | Pass → Good bar | Est. length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Executive summary | State the problem and the headline recommendation | — | Good: the reader can state your recommendation and its justification in one sentence after reading only this | 0.5 page |
| 1 | Company & supply chain map | Map the Panama → Moín → Vlissingen lane | SCOR | Good: the map shows *where* the risk sits, not just *that* Chiquita exists | 1–1.5 pages |
| 2 | Problem statement | State the resilience hypothesis and scope | — | Good: problem is specific and falsifiable, not a topic | 0.5 page |
| 3 | Tactical/operational analysis | Show which components hinder resilient international operation | GVC governance, bullwhip effect, Porter | Pass shows findings *derive from* the analysis; **Good shows how components influence each other and justifies why these specifically** | 3–4 pages |
| 4 | Recommendations | Viable/feasible/compliant proposals, expert-substantiated | Resilience literature, compliance frameworks | Pass: expert insight incorporated. **Good: insight consciously weighed; state why this option beats the alternative, and name the alternative rejected** | 3–4 pages |
| 5 | Scenario evaluation | Weigh the 4 scenarios against the 2 rejected options | Christopher & Peck; Sheffi & Rice; Nahmias | Good: argued trade-offs, not a list of options | 1.5–2 pages |
| 6 | Implementation plan | People, processes, information | Kotter / ADKAR | Pass: three elements considered. **Good: three elements clearly interconnected in one coherent plan** | 1.5–2 pages |
| 7 | Country analysis — Panama | Geopolitical/macro factors → strategic implications | STIRDEEPER, ILO/SA8000/GLOBALG.A.P. | Pass: link established. **Good: analysis demonstrably strengthens the redesign argument — not a standalone profile** | 2–3 pages |
| 8 | Country training | Professional presentation of the Panama analysis | Country Analysis, Strategic Implications | Format still unconfirmed — see Phase 3 | Format-dependent |
| 9 | References & appendices | APA references, interview logs, GenAI declaration | — | Conditional criterion — gate, not a grading dimension | As needed |

> [!important] The one rule that decides Pass vs Good across every section
> Don't just report more findings. For every recommendation and every scenario, name what you rejected and why. That single habit is what the rubric calls "weighing considerations" and "justifying why this option is most appropriate."
