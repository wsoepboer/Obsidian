# Personal Logbook

[[00 Global Dynamics and Supply Chain Management MOC|↩ MOC]] · [[Rubric - Global Dynamics and Supply Chain Management|Rubric]]

> [!info] Source
> Brightspace *Personal logbook* module, org unit `200779`, verbatim:
> > Please make sure you track your individual contribution in a personal logbook. So you can demonstrate your contribution in the project.

## Why this is not admin
Three separate statements in the material converge on it:

- The Kick-off deck: **"Individual track"** and **"individual questions"** inside a group assessment
- The hand-out: team-performance feedback **stops on 1 October**
- The rubric: an **individual component conducted by two assessors**

The product is a team report; the grade is yours. The logbook is the only evidence you control that separates the two — and it cannot be reconstructed at the end.

## What to record, per entry
| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| **Date** | Establishes the timeline, which is what makes it credible |
| **What I did** | Concrete: mapped tier-2 suppliers, drafted the risk section, ran the interview |
| **Output** | The artefact, and where it lives |
| **With whom** | Team members, coach, expert |
| **Time** | Rough hours |
| **Decisions** | What was decided and on what grounds — feeds the *"weighs up different considerations"* criterion |
| **Blockers** | Including a team member not delivering. **Dated, before 1 October** |

## Two things to log deliberately
- **Expert interviews** — who, when, what they said, how it changed the recommendation. The rubric grades whether expert insight was *weighed and integrated*, and your notes are the proof it was yours.
- **Rejected options** — the alternatives you considered and dropped, with reasons. That is precisely the Pass → Good difference, and in the individual questioning you will be asked to defend the choice.

## Practical
Keep it as one running note per week rather than a table you tidy later — the value is the timestamped detail, not the formatting. Cross-link to the vault notes it produced so the trail is navigable.
