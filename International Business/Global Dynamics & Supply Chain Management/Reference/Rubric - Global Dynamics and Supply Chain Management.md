## The learning outcome assessed
> IB professionals make recommendations as to how the organisation should be redesigned to successfully do business on an international scale in a responsible manner. They analyse operational / tactical processes and determine which organisational elements are incompatible with doing business on an international scale in a sustainable, responsible, efficient and effective manner. They formulate viable, feasible and compliant recommendations for organisational change in consultation and collaboration with experts from various disciplines, considering appropriate current digital and technological solutions. After identifying the necessary changes and getting approval from decision makers, IB professionals design, plan, coordinate and/or monitor the operational implementation process of these changes, in terms of people, processes and information, making effective use of current digital and technological tools.. In addition, the student conducts a country analysis in which geopolitical and macro-economic factors are translated into strategic implications for organisations, and presents this analysis professionally in the form of a country training.

*(The double full stop after "digital and technological tools.." is in the original.)*

## Assessment threshold
> All conditional criteria must be met; otherwise, the maximum assessment is a near pass (5).
>
> To pass the Unit of Assessment, each part of the learning outcome must receive a grade of at least pass (6).
>
> If a learning outcome is divided into parts, each part carries equal weight unless a different weighting is specified.
>
> In accordance with the assessment plan, the final assessment is recorded as either a numerical grade or a word grade.
>
> Example of the final grade calculation when the learning outcome is divided into three parts: (grade for learning outcome part 1 + grade for learning outcome part 2 + grade for learning outcome part 3) / 3 = final grade. Example of the final grade calculation when the learning outcome is divided into three parts and part 1 carries twice the weight: ((grade for learning outcome part 1 x 2) + grade for learning outcome part 2 + grade for learning outcome part 3) / 4 = final grade.

## Learning outcome, part 1 — the organisational redesign
The criterion, as stated:

> IB professionals analyse operational / tactical processes and determine which organisational elements are incompatible with doing business on an international scale in a sustainable, responsible, efficient and effective manner.
>
> They formulate viable, feasible and compliant recommendations for redesigning organisations to successfully do business on an international scale in a responsible manner, in consultation and collaboration with experts from various disciplines and considering appropriate current digital and technological solutions.
>
> After identifying the necessary changes and getting approval from decision makers, IB professionals design, plan, coordinate and/or monitor the operational implementation process of these changes, in terms of people, processes and information, making effective use of current digital and technological tools.

### Pass — *the learning outcome has largely been achieved*
> The student identifies which organisational components hinder international, sustainable, responsible, efficient and effective business operations, and demonstrates how these findings derive from an analysis of operational and tactical processes. The reasoning is clear and coherent, and the conclusions are well substantiated.
>
> The recommendations align with the organizational needs and are appropriate within the international context in which the organization operates. The substantiation demonstrates that relevant insights from experts across various disciplines have been incorporated. The recommendations are viable, feasible, compliant with regulations, and take into account appropriate digital and technological solutions.
>
> The student develops an operational implementation process that clearly incorporates activities for designing, planning, coordinating and/or monitoring the change. Due consideration is given to people including decision makers, processes and information, and current digital and technological tools are used purposefully.

### Good — *the learning outcome has fully been achieved*
> The student identifies which organisational components hinder international, sustainable, responsible, efficient and effective business operations, and demonstrates how these components influence one another. The reasoning shows that the student establishes connections, weighs up different considerations and provides a convincing justification for why these particular components of the organisation are incompatible with successful international business.
>
> The recommendations demonstrate that insights from various disciplines have been consciously weighed and integrated into coherent, viable, feasible and regulatory-compliant recommendations. The substantiation clarifies why the chosen recommendations are most appropriate within the organization's international context. Digital and technological solutions have been purposefully selected because they reinforce the proposed organizational change.
>
> The student develops an operational implementation process in which the activities for designing, planning, coordinating and/or monitoring the change are integrated into a coherent whole. People including decision makers, processes, information and the purposeful use of current digital and technological tools are clearly interconnected.

### Excellent
> Exceeds elements of the learning outcome.

*(The document gives no separate descriptor for Excellent on this part.)*

> [!important] What separates Pass from Good — read this twice
> Three shifts, and none of them is "more findings":
>
> | | Pass | Good |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Analysis** | shows *how findings derive from* process analysis | shows *how components influence one another*, weighs considerations, justifies **why these** components |
> | **Recommendation** | expert insights *incorporated* | expert insights *consciously weighed and integrated*, and it is clear **why this option is most appropriate** |
> | **Technology** | *taken into account* | *purposefully selected because it reinforces* the change |
> | **Implementation** | people, processes, information *considered* | the three **clearly interconnected** in a coherent whole |
>
> Good is argued, not fuller. Say which alternatives you rejected and why.

## Learning outcome, part 2 — the country analysis
> In addition, the student conducts a country analysis in which geopolitical and macro-economic factors are translated into strategic implications for organisations, and presents this analysis professionally in the form of a country training.

### Pass
> The student establishes a link between geopolitical and macroeconomic factors and their strategic implications for the organisation. The country training is aligned with the analysis conducted.

### Good
> The country analysis and country training demonstrate how geopolitical and macroeconomic factors are translated into strategic implications for the organization and demonstrably strengthen the substantiation of the proposed organizational change and responsible international business.

Notes: [[Country Analysis]] · [[Country Training]] · [[Strategic Implications]]

> [!warning] Part 2 carries equal weight and has no published teaching material
> Each part must reach **at least a 6** on its own — a strong redesign cannot carry a weak country analysis. Yet Brightspace publishes no *Global Dynamics* lecture material, and the Lecture 1 schedule is labelled *"SCM only"*. **Ask for the country-training format early.**
>
> Note also what *Good* requires: the country analysis must **strengthen the substantiation of the redesign**. A standalone country profile bolted onto the report scores Pass at best; the two halves have to be one argument.

## General notes on the assessment (verbatim)
> \* The difference between largely and fully is not a matter of quantity (part or all of the learning outcome), but of quality (level of mastery, depth, breadth, complexity of the work, etc.).
>
> \*\* An assessment is always holistic. This means that examples and feedback provided to illustrate a particular assessment apply to the work as a whole. For reassessment, you must therefore analyse your entire work in light of the feedback and make adjustments where necessary, rather than addressing only the specific example mentioned.

## Purpose (verbatim)
> The purpose of the rubric is to enable learning outcomes to be assessed in a valid, reliable and transparent manner. A rubric also serves a formative purpose: it helps students understand what they are working towards. When a product or performance is assessed using the rubric, students gain insight into their abilities, which can support their learning. It also provides lecturers with information that can be used to adjust their teaching and/or guidance.
>
> In the rubrics, we use the following definitions of 'pass' and 'good':
>
> **Pass:** the student's performance largely matches the description of the learning outcome.
> **Good:** the student's performance fully meets the description of the learning outcome.
