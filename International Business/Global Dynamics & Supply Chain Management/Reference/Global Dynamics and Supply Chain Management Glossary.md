# Global Dynamics & Supply Chain Management Glossary

Every term named in the course material, one line each. [[00 Global Dynamics and Supply Chain Management MOC|↩ MOC]]

> [!info] Scope
> Built from the Brightspace material (org unit `200779`) captured 3 September 2026: the Kick-off and Lecture 1 decks, the rubric and the Extra explanation hand-out. Terms the course has **not** named are deliberately absent — see the MOC's *deliberately not written yet* list.

## 1 · Supply chain structure
- **[[Supply Chain Structure]]** — the as-is network: who is in the chain, in what order, and how goods and money move.
- **[[Supply Chain Mapping]]** — drawing that network, annotated with locations, lead times and dependencies.
- **[[Value Chain Analysis]]** — identifying where value is added and where it merely costs.
- **[[Supply Chain Actors]]** — the four roles: key suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, customers.
- **[[Tactical and Operational Level]]** — medium-term rule-setting vs daily execution; the assignment requires both.

## 2 · Sourcing and procurement
- **[[Local vs Global Sourcing]]** — the cost / lead time / risk / carbon trade-off.
- **[[Supplier Selection Criteria]]** — cost, quality and reliability, which cannot be maximised together.
- **[[Supplier Relationships and Contracts]]** — the contract makes behaviour enforceable; the relationship makes it predictable.

## 3 · Supplier management
- **[[Supplier Performance Measurement]]** — agreed measures of delivery, quality and responsiveness, acted on.
- **[[Supplier Audits]]** — inspecting the process behind the output, including labour and environmental practice.
- **[[Supplier Diversification]]** — spreading supply so one failure is not the company's failure.
- **[[Critical Supplier Dependency]]** — a supplier is critical when its failure stops you, not when it is large.
- **[[Raw Material Scarcity]]** — material shortages as a constraint on the sustainability transition itself.

## 4 · Logistics and distribution
- **[[Transportation Modes]]** — road, rail, sea, air: the speed / cost / carbon triangle.
- **[[Warehousing and Inventory Locations]]** — where stock sits: central pooling vs in-market service.
- **[[Distribution Channels]]** — route to market, and how much of the demand signal survives it.
- **[[Cross-Border Logistics]]** — customs, duties and documentation; variability more than cost.
- **[[Environmental Impact of Logistics]]** — transport as the chain's main emissions source, and the levers on it.

## 5 · Inventory management
- **[[Safety Stock]]** — stock held against uncertainty in demand, supply and lead time.
- **[[Demand Forecasting]]** — predicting demand; a distribution, not a number.
- **[[Inventory Turnover]]** — how often stock is sold and replaced; capital efficiency.
- **[[JIT vs Just-in-Case]]** — minimal stock and fragility vs buffered stock and cost.

## 6 · Risk management
- **[[Geopolitical Risk]]** — state action and instability; not diversifiable within a country.
- **[[Supply Disruption]]** — interruption of flow; severity set by concentration and thinness.
- **[[Trade Barriers and Tariffs]]** — duties plus quotas, standards and procedure; changeable overnight.
- **[[Exchange Rate Risk]]** — margin moving with the currency; hedged financially or structurally.
- **[[Climate and Disaster Risk]]** — acute events and chronic shifts, managed differently.

## 7 · Sustainability and responsible business
- **[[Ethical Sourcing]]** — accounting for how goods were produced, several tiers up.
- **[[Human Rights in the Supply Chain]]** — labour conditions and the due-diligence duty over them.
- **[[Carbon Footprint]]** — chain emissions, mostly outside the company's own operations.
- **[[Circular Economy Practices]]** — reuse, repair, remanufacture, recycle; a reverse flow and a supply strategy.
- **[[ESG and CSRD]]** — non-financial performance, and EU value-chain sustainability reporting.

## 8 · Digitalisation and technology
- **[[ERP Systems]]** — one integrated record across functions; the base the rest depends on.
- **[[Supply Chain Management Software]]** — planning and coordination *across* companies, not within one.
- **[[AI and Predictive Analytics]]** — predicting rather than reporting; weak where there is no history.
- **[[Blockchain and Traceability]]** — verifiable chain-of-custody; guarantees the record, not the truth.
- **[[IoT and Real-Time Monitoring]]** — sensors giving position and condition; visibility as a substitute for stock.

## 9 · Quality management
- **[[Quality Standards and Certification]]** — ISO 9001 and the like; certifies the system, not the product.
- **[[Supplier Quality Assurance]]** — from inspecting arrivals to qualifying the supplier's process.
- **[[Product Traceability]]** — following a unit back to its inputs and forward to its destination.
- **[[Defect Prevention and Continuous Improvement]]** — acting on causes, on a cycle.

## 10 · Performance measurement
- **[[Supply Chain KPIs]]** — the seven measures the deck names; never read one alone.
- **[[On-Time-In-Full]]** — delivered on the promised date *and* complete.
- **[[Lead Time]]** — order to receipt; variability matters more than length.
- **[[Order Fulfilment Rate]]** — share of demand met from stock.
- **[[Logistics Costs]]** — total cost to move and hold, including tied-up capital.
- **[[Carbon Emissions per Shipment]]** — emissions as a performance measure alongside cost and service.

## The Global Dynamics half
- **[[Country Analysis]]** — geopolitical and macro-economic factors for one country in the network.
- **[[Country Training]]** — the required delivery format; must strengthen the redesign argument.
- **[[Strategic Implications]]** — translating a fact into a decision this company must take.

## How advice is judged
- **[[Viable Feasible Compliant]]** — the rubric's three tests, plus the implicit fourth: *why this one*.
- **[[Implementation - People Processes Information]]** — the third of the learning outcome, and the one students omit.
- **[[Expert Consultation]]** — experts from *various disciplines*, weighed rather than quoted.

## Course machinery
- **Beer Game** — the simulation run on 2 September. The deck names it and explains nothing; ask what the takeaway was.
- **4-eye policy** — every assessment conducted by two assessors.
- **Individual track** — the individually assessed component inside the group product → [[Personal Logbook]].
- **GO meeting** — 11 September, where each group's plan is approved or not → [[The Supply Chain Assignment]].
- **Conditional criteria** — pass/fail gates; missing one caps the grade at 5 → [[Rubric - Global Dynamics and Supply Chain Management]].
