The brief, the plan and the deadlines. [[00 Global Dynamics and Supply Chain Management MOC|↩ MOC]] · [[Rubric - Global Dynamics and Supply Chain Management|Rubric]]

## The brief
> Working in a team, you will select a company and map its supply chain network, or a specific part of it.
>
> Based on the current situation, you will investigate how the company can improve its existing supply chain network to become **more sustainable, faster, more effective or more cost-efficient**. You will address this at both **tactical and operational levels**. Clearly identify the problem you intend to solve for the company.
>
> Your recommendations must be well substantiated using **academic literature and relevant models**, as well as **interviews with senior experts in supply chain management**. You are also encouraged to develop **different scenarios** and/or critically reflect on your recommendations **from multiple perspectives**.
>
> Your team will also develop a proposal for **implementing** the recommended changes, taking into account **people, processes and information**.
>
> Finally, you are expected to incorporate your knowledge of **Global Dynamics** into your recommendations. **At least one country** within the supply chain network should be examined in greater depth, applying the insights and knowledge provided in the Global Dynamics component of the course.

## What is actually being asked
Six deliverable moves, each traceable to a rubric line:

| # | What | Rubric hook |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Map** the chain, or one part of it | Part 1 — analyse operational/tactical processes |
| 2 | **Name the problem** you will solve | *"Clearly identify the problem"* |
| 3 | **Recommend**, substantiated by literature, models and **expert interviews** | *"insights from experts across various disciplines"* |
| 4 | **Weigh alternatives** — scenarios, multiple perspectives | The Pass → Good gap |
| 5 | **Implementation proposal** — people, processes, information | Part 1, third paragraph |
| 6 | **Country depth** on ≥1 country, as a country training | Part 2 |

> The hand-out writes both. `00:00` on Wednesday 9 September means the plan is due as **Tuesday night ends**; `12:00` means midday Wednesday. The Kick-off deck says only *"by Wednesday 9 September at 12:00"*. **Work to the earlier reading** and confirm with Chiel.

## Choosing the company
> As mentioned, you may choose a company from your own network, but you may also select a company based on your own research.

No size threshold, sector or country restriction is stated. The only implied constraints: the chain must be mappable, senior SCM experts must be reachable, and at least one country in the network must be worth a deep analysis.

## The GO meeting — Fri 11 September
> On Friday 11 September, we will discuss the assignment with each group and determine whether you receive a **GO**. Make sure you have developed your ideas as much as possible in advance.

A gate, not a check-in. Come with a company, a stated problem, and named experts you have already approached.

## Team performance — the 1 October cut-off
> Feedback regarding team performance will no longer be addressed after 1 October. Therefore, make sure you create deliverables in good time so that you can assess whether all team members are contributing. In terms of planning, I strongly recommend bringing your own internal deadlines forward rather than working towards the final deadlines.

Read plainly: if a team member is not contributing, **raise it before 1 October** — after that you carry it. This is what the [[Personal Logbook]] is for.
