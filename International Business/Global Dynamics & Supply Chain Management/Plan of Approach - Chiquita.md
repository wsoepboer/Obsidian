# Plan of Approach — Chiquita Brands International

**Global Dynamics & Supply Chain Management** · Group 3 · *[team members]*
Two-page plan, due **Wednesday 9 September 2026, 12:00**

## 1. The company

**Chiquita Brands International** — banana and fresh-produce multinational, private since the Cutrale–Safra acquisition completed in January 2015 (PR Newswire, 2015). It sources from owned farms and contracted growers across Panama, Costa Rica, Honduras, Guatemala, Colombia, Ecuador, Nicaragua, Mexico and Peru (Food Republic, 2025); at its last public disclosure roughly **one-third** of volume came from owned farms, and its three largest independent growers supplied about 22% of purchased volume (Chiquita Brands International, 2015). Fruit reaches Europe by owned and chartered reefer vessels in controlled-atmosphere containers, with a weekly arrival at Vlissingen (Chiquita Brands, n.d.). Since the 2025 Panama shutdown, Bocas del Toro fruit has been exported through Costa Rica's Port of Moín (Fruitnet, 2026).

**[D] Scope** — the **Panama → Port of Moín → Vlissingen / North-West European retail** lane, not the whole global network, so that operational detail is real rather than illustrative.

**[D] Improvement direction** — primarily **more effective** (origin resilience), with **more sustainable** as joint driver.

**[A] Feasibility** — the chain is mappable, since we have already mapped it. Expert access is assumed, not secured: we rely on the Vlissingen touchpoint and the course's CNV Internationaal partnership. If neither converts into a senior interview, the company choice weakens.

## 2. The problem

### 2.1 Hypothesis — **[A] ours, not a finding**

> Chiquita's origin stage concentrates volume in single production zones under a labour relationship it does not control, while the product permits almost no inventory buffer. A disruption at one origin therefore becomes *permanently* lost volume, because buyers of a perishable reallocate rather than wait.

No source states this causal link. Testing it is the analytical core of the report.

### 2.2 The documented facts it rests on

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| Chiquita controlled ~90% of Panama's banana production pre-2025, traceable to one zone, Bocas del Toro | Food Republic, 2025 |
| The 2025 SITRAIBANA strike, triggered by pension reform (Law 462), halted ~90% of national output; $75m direct losses on Chiquita's estimate, some local media over $100m; 6,000+ dismissals with alleged ILO 87/98 breaches; $30m to restart, exports resuming February 2026 | Al Jazeera, 2025; Banana Link, 2025b; FreshPlaza, n.d.-b; Fruitnet, 2026; IntelliNews, 2025 |
| Chiquita could not export more than 900,000 boxes during the shutdown, and buyers began substituting to other origins (Bonita, Turbana) | Banana Link, 2025a |
| Green bananas ripen to order in five to nine days — no finished-goods buffer exists | FreshPlaza, n.d.-a |
| Established plants bear fruit roughly every **7** months; destroyed plantings take about **9** months to bear again | Chiquita Brands International, 2015 |
| Cavendish is ~99% of traded bananas with no commercial TR4 resistance; TR4 confirmed in Ecuador in December 2025 and again July 2026; Chiquita's resistant variety targets 2028–30 | Ecofin Agency, 2025; FreshFruitPortal, 2026a, 2026b |
| EU duty €114/tonne MFN against €75/tonne for Andean and Central American origins | European Commission, n.d. |
| Plantation workers take ~7% of retail price against retailers' ~41%; over 80% of the Panamanian plantation workforce is Ngäbe-Buglé | FreshPlaza, n.d.-c; Bartel et al., n.d. |

### 2.3 **[E] Candidate risks — to be tested**

Deliberately broader than the evidence above. For each, the research must establish whether it is material *and* whether Chiquita already controls for it; a risk found to be well managed is a finding, telling us where not to recommend.

| Candidate risk | What the research must establish |
|---|---|
| Fruit quality and green-life loss in storage, shipping and ripening | Real shrink and rejection rates, and whether controlled-atmosphere shipping and ripening-to-order already absorb them |
| Trade-route and conflict disruption | Whether this chain has any documented lane exposure at all — our research so far evidences tariff and origin risk only, so this may prove immaterial |
| Tariff and trade-policy change | Whether the ~€39/tonne preference margin is under review, and how exposed the origin mix is to a change |
| Tightening wage, safety and human-rights standards at origin | Whether rising statutory standards raise origin cost materially, and whether SA8000/GLOBALG.A.P. already cover what regulation will require |
| TR4 progression into Central America | Whether spread is likely: outbreaks remain quarantined and Ecuadorian exports rose 5.5% in H1 2026 (FreshFruitPortal, 2026b). We assign no likelihood of our own |
| Recurrence of origin labour disputes | Whether the post-restart sharecropping model lowers the risk or merely relocates it |

**Contested figure.** Panama's banana export dependency is given as both 17% of Q1-2025 export revenue and 12% of January–May 2025 exports (FreshPlaza, n.d.-b; Tico Times, 2025) — irreconcilable as published. We will resolve it against Panama's national trade statistics and use neither until we have.

### 2.4 Both required levels

**Tactical** — sourcing geography, contract form, origin redundancy, and siting of ripening and cold-chain capacity. **Operational** — allocation and routing when an origin drops out, ripening-schedule and green-life management, and how origin labour and biosecurity conditions are actually observed rather than merely certified.

## 3. Literature, experts and insights

**Diagnosis, applied.** SCOR (Association for Supply Chain Management, 2022); global value-chain governance (Gereffi et al., 2005), under which we read Chiquita as a hierarchy/captive hybrid; the bullwhip effect (Lee et al., 1997) in a perishable chain with no buffer; Porter (2008); and STIRDEEPER for the macro scan (Paniagua R., 2024).

**[E] Redesign — identified, not yet worked through.** Christopher and Peck (2004) and Sheffi and Rice (2005) on resilience and the redundancy-versus-flexibility trade-off; perishable-inventory and postponement literature (Nahmias, 1982, onward), to test whether green-life extension can substitute for buffer stock. For the compliance test: CSRD, the CSDDD as it currently stands, ILO Conventions 87 and 98, SA8000 and GLOBALG.A.P. — **[D]** read in primary text, not secondary reporting. **[E] Implementation:** Kotter or ADKAR mapped onto people, processes and information, with decision-maker approval as an explicit step.

**Experts — none interviewed yet; status to update before the GO meeting.** Labour and human rights: CNV Internationaal (course partner), Banana Link. Senior SCM practice: a Chiquita or peer-importer supply-chain manager via the Vlissingen operation, and a reefer or cold-chain operator. Plant health and continuity: Wageningen University & Research (Yelloway partner), CORBANA. Trade and compliance: a customs or trade-policy adviser. Finance: Research Group Supply Chain Finance (course partner). At least one interview will be with a **senior supply-chain management practitioner**, as the brief requires; the rest supply the "various disciplines" the rubric weighs.

**[D] Country in depth: Panama** — on the strike and its trigger, the ~90% single-zone concentration, the post-restart sharecropping model, bananas as the country's leading export (Al Jazeera, 2025), and the Ngäbe-Buglé workforce (Bartel et al., n.d.). **[E]** The country training will be built to strengthen the redesign argument, not to sit beside it.

**[E] Scenarios to weigh.** Origin diversification and nearshoring — the October 2025 Sicilian plantation is documented (Blue News, 2025), while its scalability is our speculation; relational governance at origin (co-investment, living-wage contracting, union recognition), which lowers the probability of disruption rather than exposure to it; varietal and biosecurity transition across the 2028–30 bridge; cold-chain postponement. Expected rejections, with reasons: exit from a source country, threatened in 2025 and reversed (Fruitnet, 2025), and buffer stock, which perishability forecloses.

**Declaration on the use of GenAI.** Claude Opus 5 (Anthropic) was used for source verification, structuring and APA formatting. All analytical claims, the problem hypothesis, the scoping decisions and the source selection are the authors'. Every factual claim above is traceable to a cited source, and each AI-proposed change was individually reviewed before inclusion.
