# T1 — Foundation of Supply Chain Management

**Topic 1 · Global Dynamics & Supply Chain Management** — [[00 Global Dynamics and Supply Chain Management MOC|↩ Course MOC]] · [[Global Dynamics & Supply Chain Management - Course Info|Course Info]]
**From:** [[2026-09-02 L01 - Foundation of Supply Chain Management]]

## Overview
The only theory published for this course, and it is a single well-made structure: **ten pillars of supply chain management**, each with a **key question** about operating internationally. Learn the ten questions and you have both a revision framework and the diagnostic grid for the assignment.

Read the pillars as a chain in two senses. Physically they follow the goods — structure, then sourcing, then suppliers, then transport, then stock. Then four pillars cut across all of it: risk, sustainability, technology, quality. The tenth closes the loop by measuring whether any of it works.

## Learning objectives
- Name the ten pillars and the key question each one asks
- Map a real company onto the ten pillars and locate where its problem sits
- Distinguish the **tactical** from the **operational** level of a supply chain problem
- Connect each pillar to what changes when the company operates internationally

## Using the ten pillars on the assignment
1. Walk the company through all ten and answer each key question, however briefly.
2. The pillars where the answer is weakest are the candidate problems.
3. Pick one, state it as a problem — the brief says *"clearly identify the problem you intend to solve"*.
4. Place it at **tactical** and **operational** level → [[Tactical and Operational Level]].
5. Choose the improvement direction the brief offers: **more sustainable · faster · more effective · more cost-efficient**.
6. Attach a KPI from pillar 10 so the improvement is demonstrable.

## Self-test
1. Name all ten pillars in order, and the key question attached to each.
2. Which pillar does *"Is the current supply chain suitable for change?"* belong to, and why is that question first?
3. What is the difference between pillar 2 (sourcing and procurement) and pillar 3 (supplier management)?
4. A company sources a single critical component from one supplier in one country. Which pillars does that touch, and what would you measure?
5. Give the four improvement directions the assignment allows, and one KPI that would evidence each.
6. Why does the deck put sustainability among the ten pillars rather than treating it as a constraint?
7. Which pillar is assessed twice by the rubric, and where in the rubric?
8. **JIT vs Just-in-Case** — state the trade-off, and what entering a distant new market does to it.
9. For pillar 6, name the five risk categories the deck lists.
10. Why is the tenth pillar the one that makes the other nine auditable?

*Answers: work them out from the pillar sections above — every one is answerable from this note.*
