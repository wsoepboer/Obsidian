## How the course is built
The published material is thin on theory and heavy on demands, and that is itself the design:

1. **The ten pillars are a diagnostic grid.** Each pillar ends in a *key question* about international operations. Run a company through all ten and you have located the problem worth solving.
2. **The assignment then narrows it.** You pick one problem, at **tactical and operational level**, and improve one of four things: sustainability, speed, effectiveness, cost.
3. **The rubric decides what counts as advice.** Not a description of the chain — a recommendation that is *viable, feasible, compliant*, built with **experts from various disciplines**, and accompanied by an **implementation plan in terms of people, processes and information**.
4. **Global Dynamics supplies the outside.** At least one country in the network is examined in depth and presented as a **country training** — geopolitical and macro-economic factors turned into strategic implications.

Two habits the material rewards:

- **Say what you would not do.** The rubric's *Good* level is about weighing alternatives and justifying the choice, not about listing more findings.
- **Connect people, processes and information.** *Pass* asks you to consider all three; *Good* asks that they be **interconnected** in one coherent implementation.
