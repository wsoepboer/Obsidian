# Warehousing and Inventory Locations

Pillar 4's second bullet, and a decision about **geography** rather than quantity: not how much stock, but where it sits.

Central warehousing pools demand, so total stock is lower and utilisation higher — at the cost of longer delivery distances to the customer. Regional or in-market warehousing shortens delivery and improves service, at the cost of duplicated stock, more fixed cost and more complexity. The pattern most international companies converge on is a hybrid: slow-moving and high-value centrally, fast-moving locally.

Entering a new market forces the question. Serve it from the existing hub and lead times are long but no capital is committed; place stock in-market and you have committed before demand is proven. That is a genuine scenario choice, which the assignment explicitly encourages you to model.
