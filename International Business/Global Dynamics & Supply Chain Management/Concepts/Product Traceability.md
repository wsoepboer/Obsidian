# Product Traceability

Pillar 9's third bullet. Being able to follow a specific unit both ways: **backwards** to its inputs, batch and supplier, and **forwards** to where it went.

Four uses, and they explain why this concept keeps reappearing across the ten pillars:

- **Recall** — a traceable chain recalls one batch; an untraceable one recalls everything, at ruinous cost
- **Root cause** — a defect can be tied to a supplier, batch or process step → [[Defect Prevention and Continuous Improvement]]
- **Compliance and claims** — origin, certification and sustainability assertions become evidenced rather than asserted → [[Ethical Sourcing]], [[ESG and CSRD]]
- **Counterfeit control** — provenance you can verify

It requires identification at unit or batch level, records at every handover, and data that survives crossing company boundaries — which is where [[Blockchain and Traceability]] and [[IoT and Real-Time Monitoring]] are proposed as mechanisms.

Depth is the honest limit: most chains trace tier 1 well and tier 3 barely, and that gap is where reputational risk lives.
