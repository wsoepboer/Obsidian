# Local vs Global Sourcing

**Pillar 2's first bullet**, and the trade-off the whole pillar turns on.

| | Local | Global |
|---|---|---|
| Unit cost | Usually higher | Usually lower |
| Lead time | Short | Long |
| Flexibility | High | Low |
| Risk exposure | Lower | Geopolitical, currency, disruption |
| Carbon | Lower transport emissions | Higher |
| Oversight | Easy to audit | Costly to audit |

Cheaper per unit is not cheaper per chain: a long-haul supplier buys its price advantage with [[Safety Stock]], longer [[Lead Time]], currency exposure and thinner control over conditions upstream.

> **Pillar key question: Does the sourcing strategy support international expansion?**

Which reframes it — the question is not *local or global* in the abstract, but whether the current sourcing pattern can serve the markets the company wants to enter.
