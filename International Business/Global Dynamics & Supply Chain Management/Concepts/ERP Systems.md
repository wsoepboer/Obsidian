# ERP Systems

**Pillar 8's first bullet.** Enterprise Resource Planning: one integrated system and one shared data model across finance, procurement, production, inventory and sales.

Its supply chain value is not any single function but the removal of reconciliation — stock, orders, capacity and cost read from the same record, so a decision in one function is visible in the others. That is the precondition for most of the rest of the pillar: forecasting, analytics and monitoring all need a trustworthy transactional base.

The limits are worth stating, because "implement an ERP" is a recommendation students make too casually. It is expensive, slow, and its failures are organisational rather than technical — processes have to change to fit it, master data has to be cleaned, and people have to use it as intended. In rubric terms that is squarely the **people, processes and information** implementation question → [[Implementation - People Processes Information]].
