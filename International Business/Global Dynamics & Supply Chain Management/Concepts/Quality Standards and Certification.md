# Quality Standards and Certification

**Pillar 9's first bullet**, and the deck names one example: **ISO 9001**.

A standard is an agreed specification for how quality is managed; certification is an accredited third party confirming the specification is followed. ISO 9001 certifies a **quality management system** — documented processes, defined responsibilities, records, corrective action, continuous improvement — and notably not the product itself. That distinction matters: a certified supplier has a system, which makes good output likely and not guaranteed.

Why it is in a supply chain course rather than an operations one: certification is how quality is made **transferable across borders**. It lets a buyer place trust in a supplier it cannot easily visit, satisfies customers and regulators in destination markets, and often functions as a precondition to bid at all.

> **Pillar key question: How can quality be maintained across international operations?**

Certification is the scalable half of the answer; [[Supplier Audits]] is the half that verifies it.
