# Blockchain and Traceability

Pillar 8's fourth bullet, paired by the deck with traceability — which is the only pairing in which it is worth discussing.

The proposition: a shared, append-only record across parties who do not fully trust each other, so provenance and chain-of-custody claims can be verified rather than asserted. That maps onto real needs — origin claims for [[Ethical Sourcing]], certification integrity, and the value-chain data that [[ESG and CSRD]] requires.

Where it is genuinely useful is multi-party, low-trust, high-verification-value chains: food safety, pharmaceuticals, conflict-exposed minerals, luxury authenticity.

State the limitation plainly, because it is the mark of a well-argued recommendation: a ledger guarantees that a record was **not altered**, not that it was **true when entered**. Garbage in stays garbage, immutably. So blockchain complements physical verification — [[Supplier Audits]], sensors — rather than replacing it, and in a single-company chain a database is usually the honest answer.
