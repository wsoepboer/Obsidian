# Supplier Relationships and Contracts

Pillar 2's third bullet. The relationship and the contract are two instruments for the same problem — making a supplier's behaviour predictable — and they work differently.

- **The contract** fixes what is enforceable: price, volume, lead time, quality standards, liability, exit. It protects you when things go wrong.
- **The relationship** determines what happens *before* things go wrong: whether you hear about a shortage early, get priority in an allocation, or are brought into a redesign.

Transactional buying maximises short-term price and forfeits the second. Partnership arrangements trade some price flexibility for information, priority and joint improvement — which is why they matter more the longer and thinner the chain.

Contracts are also where compliance lives: audit rights, human-rights and ESG clauses, traceability obligations. A recommendation that requires supplier behaviour to change usually requires a contract clause to make it real.
