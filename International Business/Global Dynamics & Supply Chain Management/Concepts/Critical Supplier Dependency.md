# Critical Supplier Dependency

Pillar 3's fourth bullet: *"dependency on critical suppliers."* A supplier is critical when its failure stops the company — not when it is large.

That distinction is what makes the analysis worth doing. A low-spend supplier of one irreplaceable component is more critical than the biggest line in the procurement budget. The tests are practical: could this input be sourced elsewhere, how long would qualifying an alternative take, and what stops production in the meantime?

Dependency compounds when it is invisible: a tier-2 or tier-3 supplier shared by several of your "independent" tier-1 suppliers is a single point of failure you cannot see on a tier-1 map.

The lecture puts this at a continental scale — *"Europe's transition to a sustainable society is at risk due to a shortage of raw materials"* → [[Raw Material Scarcity]].

Responses: [[Supplier Diversification]] · [[Safety Stock]] · long-term contracts · qualifying an alternative before you need it.
