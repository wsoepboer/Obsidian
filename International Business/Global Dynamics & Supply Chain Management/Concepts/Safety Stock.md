# Safety Stock

**Pillar 5.** Stock held not to meet expected demand but to absorb **uncertainty** — in demand, in supply, and in lead time.

Two variables drive how much is needed: how variable demand is, and how long and how variable the replenishment lead time is. Both get worse with distance, which is why the pillar's key question is framed the way it is.

> **Pillar key question: How should inventory policies change when entering new markets?**

Answer: usually upward at first, because a new market has no demand history and often a longer supply line. Safety stock is the price of not knowing yet.

It is also the substitutable term in several other decisions. Cheaper distant sourcing, slower sea freight and central warehousing all lower one cost and raise this one. A recommendation that ignores the offset is incomplete — and the offset is visible in [[Inventory Turnover]].
