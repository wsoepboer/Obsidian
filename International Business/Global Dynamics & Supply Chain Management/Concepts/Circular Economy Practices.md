# Circular Economy Practices

Pillar 7's fourth bullet, and a word the lecture flags separately — one framing slide reads simply **"Circular · More complexibility · More independance"**, which is the argument in three words.

A linear chain runs take → make → use → discard. A circular one keeps materials in play through reuse, repair, refurbishment, remanufacture and recycling, and designs products so that is possible.

The supply chain consequence is a **reverse flow**: goods coming back, which needs collection, sorting, inspection, and returns logistics that most networks were never built for. Hence *more complexity*.

And hence *more independence* — recovered material is material you do not have to buy from a concentrated, politically exposed source. That is why [[Raw Material Scarcity]] makes circularity a supply strategy and not only an environmental one, and why it can be argued on resilience grounds to a company that is indifferent to the environmental case.
