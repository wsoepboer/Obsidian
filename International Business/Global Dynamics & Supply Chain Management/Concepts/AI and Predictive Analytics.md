# AI and Predictive Analytics

Pillar 8's third bullet. Using historical and live data to predict rather than to report: demand, lead-time variability, supplier failure, maintenance needs, and route or price movements.

Where it earns its place in a chain: forecasting with more variables than a human can weigh ([[Demand Forecasting]]), anomaly detection that flags a deviation before it becomes a stockout, scenario simulation for the *"different scenarios"* the assignment asks for, and prescriptive optimisation of routes and inventory levels.

Three honest caveats to include if you recommend it. It needs data volume and data quality that many mid-sized companies do not have — which usually makes [[ERP Systems]] the prerequisite. It predicts from the past, so it handles a **new market** poorly, exactly where the uncertainty is highest. And a model nobody trusts changes no decisions, which returns the problem to people and processes.

The rubric's *Good* wording applies here more than anywhere: technology chosen *because it reinforces* the change, not because it is current.
