# Strategic Implications

The verb in part 2 of the learning outcome — geopolitical and macro-economic factors *"translated into strategic implications for organisations"* — and the step most country analyses skip.

The move is from **fact** to **consequence for this company**:

| Fact | Not an implication | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation at 40% | "Inflation is high" | Local-currency contracts erode within a quarter; price in EUR or index them |
| Export licences on a key mineral | "Trade policy is restrictive" | The single-source input needs a qualified alternative outside the jurisdiction, before the next licensing round |
| Port congestion, weak rail | "Infrastructure is poor" | Lead-time variability makes JIT untenable; hold buffer stock in-market |

Three tests for whether you have actually translated: it names a **decision**, it could be **wrong** (so it is a claim, not a description), and it changes something in the redesign you are recommending.

That last test is exactly the rubric's *Good* criterion — the analysis must *strengthen the substantiation* of the proposed change.
