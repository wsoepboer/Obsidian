# JIT vs Just-in-Case

Pillar 5's explicit trade-off — the deck writes it as *"Just-in-Time (JIT) versus Just-in-Case approaches"*.

- **Just-in-Time** — stock arrives as it is needed. Minimal capital, minimal warehousing, minimal obsolescence. Requires short, reliable lead times, dependable suppliers and good information. Fragile by design: a single disruption stops production.
- **Just-in-Case** — stock is held against uncertainty. Absorbs disruption, protects service, costs capital, space and obsolescence risk.

It is a **spectrum, not a choice of two**, and the honest position is per product line: JIT for stable, high-volume, locally supplied items; Just-in-Case for critical, hard-to-source or long-lead items → [[Critical Supplier Dependency]].

The direction of travel is worth naming in a report. Decades of JIT optimisation ran into a series of disruptions that punished it, and much of what the course calls resilience — the subject of the 18 September guest lecture — is a deliberate step back toward Just-in-Case for selected inputs.
