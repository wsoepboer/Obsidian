# Raw Material Scarcity

The live example the lecture attaches to pillar 3, quoted from the slide:

> Europe's transition to a sustainable society is at risk due to a shortage of raw materials.

The claim is worth unpacking because it inverts the usual framing. Sustainability is normally a constraint *on* supply chains; here the supply chain is a constraint *on* sustainability. Electrification, batteries, wind and grid infrastructure all require materials whose extraction and refining are concentrated in a small number of countries — so the greener the target, the more material-dependent and more geopolitically exposed the route to it.

For a company this shows up as [[Critical Supplier Dependency]] it did not choose and cannot easily diversify away, and as a reason that [[Circular Economy Practices]] — recovery, reuse, recycling — is a supply strategy rather than a reputational one.

For the assignment: if the company's chain touches such materials, this belongs in both the risk pillar and the sustainability pillar, and it is a natural bridge into the [[Country Analysis]].
