# Ethical Sourcing

**Pillar 7's first bullet.** Buying in a way that accounts for how the goods were produced — labour conditions, pay, safety, environmental practice, and the integrity of the purchasing relationship itself.

The difficulty is structural rather than moral. Companies are held responsible for conditions **several tiers upstream**, where they have no contract, often no visibility, and limited leverage. So ethical sourcing in practice means building the machinery to see and influence what you do not directly control: supplier codes of conduct, audit rights written into contracts, traceability, and remediation rather than immediate delisting → [[Supplier Audits]], [[Product Traceability]].

Two failure modes to name in a report: treating a certificate as evidence of conditions, and cutting off a failing supplier in a way that harms the workers the policy was meant to protect.

The course puts weight here — **CNV International**, a trade-union development organisation, is a named guest lecturer, and *responsible* is in the learning outcome.
