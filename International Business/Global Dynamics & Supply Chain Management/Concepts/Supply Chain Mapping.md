# Supply Chain Mapping

The technique named in pillar 1 and the first thing the assignment asks for: draw the network — nodes, flows, tiers — so that the problem has somewhere to sit.

A map is not an org chart. It shows, at minimum: each actor and its role, the direction and volume of goods flowing between them, where the goods physically sit and wait, which borders they cross, and which links are single-threaded. The information and money flows run alongside and often in the opposite direction.

What makes a map analytically useful rather than decorative is that it is **annotated**: lead times on the arrows, locations on the nodes, dependencies marked. An unannotated map supports no conclusions.

Scope is yours to set — the brief allows *"or a specific part of it"* — but say explicitly where you drew the boundary and why.
