# Human Rights in the Supply Chain

Pillar 7's second bullet, and the substance behind [[Ethical Sourcing]]: forced and child labour, freedom of association, safe working conditions, working hours, living wage, and non-discrimination — including for the migrant and agency workers who are hardest to see in an audit.

What has changed is the legal status. Human-rights **due diligence** is increasingly a duty rather than a policy: identify risks in your own chain, act on them, and report on what you found → [[ESG and CSRD]]. That converts a reputational question into a compliance question, and *compliant* is one of the three words the rubric uses for a good recommendation.

Practically it means risk-mapping the chain by country and sector, prioritising where exposure is highest, securing audit and remedy mechanisms contractually, and providing grievance channels for workers rather than only reporting lines for buyers.

**CNV International** — the named guest lecturer — works precisely on this, so expect it examined.
