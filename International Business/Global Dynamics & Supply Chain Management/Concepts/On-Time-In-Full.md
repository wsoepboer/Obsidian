# On-Time-In-Full

Pillar 10's first named KPI. The share of orders delivered **both** on the promised date **and** complete.

The conjunction is the whole idea. Measured separately, on-time and in-full each look respectable while the customer's actual experience — one delivery, right day, everything there — fails. OTIF is deliberately unforgiving: a shipment 95% complete scores zero.

It is the headline service measure, and it is the natural counterweight in any cost- or stock-reduction recommendation. Cut inventory, consolidate warehouses or switch to a slower mode, and OTIF is where the damage shows first → [[Safety Stock]], [[Transportation Modes]].

Two practical cautions worth a sentence in a report: it depends on what "on time" is measured against — the date the customer requested or the date the company confirmed, which are not the same thing and the difference flatters the seller. And it says nothing about *why* a miss happened, so it needs a cause breakdown behind it to be actionable.
