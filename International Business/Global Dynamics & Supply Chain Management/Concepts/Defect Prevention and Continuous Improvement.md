# Defect Prevention and Continuous Improvement

Pillar 9's fourth bullet, and the pillar's underlying logic: quality is cheapest where it is built in, most expensive where it is inspected out, and catastrophic where the customer finds it.

Prevention means acting on causes rather than symptoms — capable processes, mistake-proofing, root-cause analysis on every escape, and specifications suppliers can actually meet. Continuous improvement means doing this on a **cycle** rather than after incidents: measure, find the cause, change the process, verify, standardise.

Two things make it credible in a report. It is measurable — defect and rejection rates, cost of poor quality, rework hours — so it fits pillar 10. And it is an **organisational** change, not a technical one: it needs ownership, training and time, which is exactly the *people, processes, information* framing the rubric grades → [[Implementation - People Processes Information]].

The international dimension: prevention has to be transferred to suppliers and sites you do not manage, which is why it depends on the rest of pillar 9.
