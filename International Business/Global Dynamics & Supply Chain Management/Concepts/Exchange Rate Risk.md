# Exchange Rate Risk

Pillar 6's fourth bullet. When a chain buys in one currency and sells in another, the margin moves with the exchange rate even if nothing operational changes.

Three forms are worth distinguishing in a report: **transaction** exposure on committed purchases and sales, **economic** exposure where a sustained move makes a supplier or a market structurally cheaper or dearer, and **translation** exposure in the accounts.

Responses split between financial and structural. Financially: forward contracts and hedges, invoicing in a chosen currency, natural offsetting of inflows and outflows. Structurally: sourcing or producing inside the currency zone you sell in — which is a supply chain decision, and the one this course cares about.

It compounds long chains. A distant supplier already carries long [[Lead Time]] and higher [[Safety Stock]]; add currency exposure over that lead time and the cheap unit price is doing less work than it appears to.
