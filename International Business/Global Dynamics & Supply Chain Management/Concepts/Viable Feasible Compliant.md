# Viable Feasible Compliant

The three words the rubric uses for a recommendation, in both the learning outcome and the Pass and Good descriptors. They are three separate tests, and a recommendation has to survive all of them.

- **Viable** — it would actually solve the problem, and it makes business sense: the benefit exceeds the cost and it fits the company's strategy and market position.
- **Feasible** — *this* company can do it, with the money, people, skills, systems, supplier relationships and time it actually has. This is where ambitious student recommendations usually fail.
- **Compliant** — it is legal and meets the regulation that applies: trade and customs rules, product and quality standards, labour and human-rights due diligence, sustainability reporting → [[ESG and CSRD]].

Then the *Good* level adds a fourth, implicit test: **why this one**. Insights *"consciously weighed and integrated"*, and *"why the chosen recommendations are most appropriate"*. Which means naming the alternatives you rejected and the grounds for rejecting them — the scenarios the assignment asks for are how you show that work.
