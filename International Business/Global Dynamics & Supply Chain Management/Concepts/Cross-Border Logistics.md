# Cross-Border Logistics

Pillar 4's fourth bullet, and where an efficient domestic chain quietly stops being efficient. Crossing a border adds customs clearance, duties and tariffs, documentation, origin rules, inspection, and product compliance in the destination market.

The operational cost is rarely the duty itself — it is **variability**. A shipment held for inspection turns a reliable lead time into a distribution with a long tail, and that tail is what has to be covered by [[Safety Stock]] or absorbed as poor [[On-Time-In-Full]] performance.

Trade agreements and blocs cut both the cost and the variability, which is why the same physical distance can be commercially very different depending on the route → [[Trade Barriers and Tariffs]].

For the assignment this is one of the cleanest bridges between the two halves of the unit: the paperwork and the policy behind it are a [[Country Analysis]] finding with an operational consequence.
