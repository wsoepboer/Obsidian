# Inventory Turnover

How many times stock is sold and replaced over a period. Named twice in the lecture — once as a pillar 5 topic and again as a pillar 10 KPI — which makes it the clearest link between an inventory policy and a measurable outcome.

High turnover means little capital tied up and low obsolescence risk, but thin cover: a disruption becomes a stockout quickly. Low turnover means the opposite — resilient, expensive, and exposed to goods ageing in a warehouse.

Its value in an advisory report is diagnostic. Turnover falling while service levels stay flat says stock is being added without benefit; turnover rising while [[Order Fulfilment Rate]] falls says the chain has been tightened past its safe point. Neither conclusion is available from either number alone.

Compare within a sector, never across: a fresh-food turnover and a capital-equipment turnover are not the same measurement.
