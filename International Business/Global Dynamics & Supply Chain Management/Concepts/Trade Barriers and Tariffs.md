# Trade Barriers and Tariffs

Pillar 6's third bullet. **Tariffs** are duties on imported goods; **non-tariff barriers** are everything else that raises the cost or difficulty of trading — quotas, licensing, standards and certification requirements, local-content rules, customs procedure.

Both change the landed cost of a sourcing or market-entry decision, and both can change **overnight** by political decision, which is why the deck files them under risk rather than under cost. A chain optimised on today's tariff schedule is a chain with a political dependency.

Practical consequences for a redesign: origin rules can make where a good is *transformed* matter more than where it is *made*; trade agreements and blocs can make a longer physical route the cheaper commercial one; and non-tariff barriers often cost more in delay than tariffs cost in money → [[Cross-Border Logistics]].
