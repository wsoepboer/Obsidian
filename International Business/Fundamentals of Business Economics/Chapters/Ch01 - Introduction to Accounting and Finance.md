# Introduction to Accounting and Finance

**Part · Financial Accounting** — [[00 Fundamentals of Business Economics MOC|↩ MOC]]
**Next:** [[Ch02 - Measuring and Reporting Financial Position]]


## Learning outcomes
- Explain the nature and roles of [[Accounting]] and [[Finance]].
- Identify the main [[Users of Accounting Information]] and their needs.
- Distinguish [[Financial Accounting]] from [[Management Accounting]].
- Explain why understanding these subjects is relevant to you.

## Core ideas
[[Accounting]] collects, analyses and communicates financial information so decision makers make **better-informed decisions**. Producing reports is a means, not an end — improving decisions is the point. This *decision-making perspective* runs through the whole book.

[[Finance]] (financial management) concerns how a business **raises funds** and **invests** them to create wealth ([[Wealth Creation]]). It weighs the cost, benefit and risk of each source of finance and appraises investment opportunities.

## Users
[[Users of Accounting Information]] are **internal** (managers) or **external** ([[Stakeholders]] such as owners, lenders, suppliers, customers, government, employees, the public). Each has distinct needs.

## Two branches
- [[Management Accounting]] — internal, detailed, frequent, unregulated, forward-looking.
- [[Financial Accounting]] — external, standardised, annual, rule-bound.

## Bigger themes
- Accounting as an **information system**: data → processing → information, worth producing only when benefits exceed costs ([[Cost-Benefit of Information]]).
- [[Wealth Creation]] over the **long term**, not maximising this year's [[Profit]].
- [[Risk and Return]] — higher returns demand higher risk.
- [[Qualities of Financial Information]] — relevance, faithful representation, comparability, verifiability.

- How do management and financial accounting differ on rules and frequency?
