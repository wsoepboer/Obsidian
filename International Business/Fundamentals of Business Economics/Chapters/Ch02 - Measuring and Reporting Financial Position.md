# Measuring and Reporting Financial Position

**Part · Financial Accounting** — [[00 Fundamentals of Business Economics MOC|↩ MOC]]
**Prev:** [[Ch01 - Introduction to Accounting and Finance]] · **Next:** [[Ch03 - Measuring and Reporting Financial Performance]]


## Learning outcomes
- Identify the three elements of the [[Statement of Financial Position]].
- Explain [[Assets]], [[Liabilities]] and [[Equity]].
- Apply the [[Fundamental Accounting Equation]].
- Prepare and interpret a statement of financial position.

## The three financial statements
- [[Statement of Cash Flows]] — cash movements in the period.
- [[Income Statement]] — wealth generated in the period.
- [[Statement of Financial Position]] — accumulated wealth at a **point in time** (this chapter).

## The statement of financial position
A snapshot of what a business **owns** ([[Assets]]), **owes** ([[Liabilities]]) and what is left for owners ([[Equity]]), built on the [[Fundamental Accounting Equation]]:

> **Assets = Liabilities + Equity**   →   **Equity = Assets − Liabilities**

- [[Assets]]: **non-current** (kept > 1 year) vs **current** (→ cash within a year).
- [[Liabilities]]: **non-current** vs **current**.
- [[Equity]]: [[Share Capital]] + [[Retained Earnings]].

## Self-test
- Restate the accounting equation to solve for equity.
- Classify: a delivery van, trade receivables, a 5-year bank loan, trade payables.
- Why must the statement always balance?
