# Korean Learning Supplement (한국어 학습 보충 자료)

Comprehensive supplement covering the writing system, pronunciation rules, vocabulary building blocks, practical sentence patterns, onomatopoeia, common learner mistakes, verb conjugation tables, and cultural context.

---

## 1. Hangul (한글): The Writing System

Hangul was created in 1443 under King Sejong the Great specifically to be easy to learn — letter shapes are designed to mimic the shape of the mouth/tongue when making that sound.

### 1.1 Basic Consonants (자음) — 14

| Hangul | Romanization | Sound Description |
|---|---|---|
| ㄱ | g/k | like "g" in "go," softer |
| ㄴ | n | like English "n" |
| ㄷ | d/t | like "d" in "dog," softer |
| ㄹ | r/l | between English "r" and "l" |
| ㅁ | m | like English "m" |
| ㅂ | b/p | like "b" in "boy," softer |
| ㅅ | s | like English "s"; becomes "sh" before ㅣ/y-vowels |
| ㅇ | (silent) / ng | silent as initial; "ng" as final consonant (batchim) |
| ㅈ | j | like "j" in "jam," softer |
| ㅊ | ch | aspirated (breathy) "ch" |
| ㅋ | k | aspirated (breathy) "k" |
| ㅌ | t | aspirated (breathy) "t" |
| ㅍ | p | aspirated (breathy) "p" |
| ㅎ | h | like English "h" |

### 1.2 Tense (Double) Consonants — 5

Made by doubling a basic letter; pronounced with tense vocal cords, no airflow burst, harder/sharper sound.

| Hangul | Romanization | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ㄲ | kk | tense "g/k" |
| ㄸ | tt | tense "d/t" |
| ㅃ | pp | tense "b/p" |
| ㅆ | ss | tense "s" |
| ㅉ | jj | tense "j" |

**Three-way consonant contrast** (common learner difficulty):
- Plain (ㄱ) vs tense (ㄲ) vs aspirated (ㅋ) produce different meanings
- 가다 (go) / 까다 (picky) / 카드 (card) all sound different in Korean
- Often collapse to "ga/ka" in English ears at first

### 1.3 Basic Vowels (모음) — 10

| Hangul | Romanization | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| ㅏ | a | "ah" |
| ㅑ | ya | "yah" |
| ㅓ | eo | "uh" (open) |
| ㅕ | yeo | "yuh" |
| ㅗ | o | "oh" |
| ㅛ | yo | "yoh" |
| ㅜ | u | "oo" |
| ㅠ | yu | "yoo" |
| ㅡ | eu | "euh" (unrounded, no English equivalent) |
| ㅣ | i | "ee" |

### 1.4 Compound Vowels — 11

Combinations of the above: ㅐ (ae, "eh"), ㅔ (e, "eh" — nearly merged with ㅐ in modern speech), ㅚ (oe), ㅟ (wi), ㅘ (wa), ㅝ (wo), ㅙ (wae), ㅞ (we), ㅢ (ui), etc.

### 1.5 Syllable Blocks: How Letters Combine

Korean is written in **syllable blocks**, not a linear string like the Latin alphabet.

Each block = 초성 (initial consonant) + 중성 (vowel) + optional 종성/받침 (final consonant, "batchim").

- **한** = ㅎ (initial) + ㅏ (vowel) + ㄴ (batchim) → "han"
- **학교** = 학 (ㅎ+ㅏ+ㄱ) + 교 (ㄱ+ㅛ) → "hakgyo" (school)

Block shape depends on vowel type:
- Vertical vowels (ㅏ,ㅓ,ㅣ) → consonant goes to the left
- Horizontal vowels (ㅗ,ㅜ,ㅡ) → consonant goes on top

## 2. Pronunciation Rules & Sound Changes (음운 변동)

This is the layer most self-taught learners skip — and the reason spoken Korean sounds different from what's written. These rules are applied automatically by native speakers and predictably.

### 2.1 Liaison (연음)

When a syllable ends in a consonant (batchim) and the next syllable starts with ㅇ (silent), the batchim "moves" to fill that silent slot.

- **한국어** → 한**구**거 (han-gu-geo, not han-guk-eo)
- **좋아요** → 조**아**요 (jo-a-yo) — the ㅎ batchim of 좋 links and softens

### 2.2 Nasalization (비음화)

A batchim ㄱ/ㄷ/ㅂ becomes nasal (ㅇ/ㄴ/ㅁ) when followed by ㄴ or ㅁ.

- **국물** → 궁물 (gung-mul, "broth") — ㄱ + ㅁ → ㅇ + ㅁ
- **십만** → 심만 (sim-man, "100,000") — ㅂ + ㅁ → ㅁ + ㅁ

### 2.3 Tensification (경음화)

A plain consonant becomes tense after a batchim ㄱ/ㄷ/ㅂ, or in certain grammatical endings.

- **학교** → hak-**kyo** (not hak-gyo) — ㄱ batchim tensifies the following ㄱ
- **있다** → it-**tta**

### 2.4 Aspiration (격음화)

ㅎ combines with neighboring ㄱ/ㄷ/ㅂ/ㅈ to produce the aspirated version ㅋ/ㅌ/ㅍ/ㅊ.

- **좋다** → 조타 (jo-ta, "good") — ㅎ + ㄷ → ㅌ
- **축하** → 추카 (chu-ka, "congratulations") — ㄱ + ㅎ → ㅋ

### 2.5 Palatalization (구개음화)

ㄷ/ㅌ batchim followed by 이/히 becomes ㅈ/ㅊ.

- **굳이** → 구지 (gu-ji, "insistently")
- **같이** → 가치 (ga-chi, "together")

### 2.6 H-Deletion (ㅎ 탈락)

ㅎ between two vowels is frequently dropped in casual speech.

- **좋아** → often heard as 조아 in fast/casual speech

### 2.7 Double Batchim (겹받침) Reading

When a syllable ends in two consonants, only one is pronounced depending on context.

- **값** (value) alone → 갑 (only ㅂ pronounced)
- **값이** (value + subject particle) → 갑씨 (ㅅ links forward and tensifies)

## 4. Practical Sentence Patterns

### 4.1 Greetings & Self-Introduction

- **안녕하세요.** — "Hello." (standard polite)
- **처음 뵙겠습니다.** — "Nice to meet you." (formal)
- **제 이름은 [이름]이에요/예요.** — "My name is ___."
- **저는 네덜란드에서 왔어요.** — "I'm from the Netherlands."

### 4.2 Ordering Food

- **여기요! / 저기요!** — "Excuse me!" (to get server's attention)
- **[메뉴] 하나 주세요.** — "One [item], please."
- **이거 주세요.** — "This one, please." (pointing)
- **물 좀 주세요.** — "Water, please."
- **계산해 주세요.** — "Check, please."

### 4.3 Asking Directions

- **[장소]가 어디예요?** — "Where is [place]?"
- **여기서 멀어요?** — "Is it far from here?"
- **어떻게 가요?** — "How do I get there?"
- **똑바로 가세요.** — "Go straight."
- **오른쪽/왼쪽으로 가세요.** — "Go right/left."

### 4.4 Phone Conversations

- **여보세요?** — "Hello?" (phone-specific)
- **지금 통화 가능하세요?** — "Can you talk now?"
- **나중에 다시 전화할게요.** — "I'll call again later."

### 4.5 Survival Phrases

- **잠깐만요.** — "Just a moment."
- **죄송합니다 / 미안해요.** — "I'm sorry." (formal/casual)
- **괜찮아요.** — "It's okay / I'm fine."
- **이해가 안 돼요.** — "I don't understand."
- **천천히 말해 주세요.** — "Please speak slowly."
- **도와주세요.** — "Please help me."

## 5. Onomatopoeia & Mimetic Words (의성어 / 의태어)

Korean has an unusually large, productive set of sound-words (의성어, mimicking actual sounds) and manner/state-words (의태어, mimicking visual states — a category English mostly lacks). Used constantly in casual speech, children's books, and comics.

| Word | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 멍멍 | 의성어 | dog bark ("woof woof") |
| 야옹 | 의성어 | cat meow |
| 쾅 | 의성어 | loud bang/slam |
| 똑똑 | 의성어 | knock-knock |
| 반짝반짝 | 의태어 | sparkling, twinkling |
| 두근두근 | 의태어 | heart pounding (nervous/excited) |
| 살금살금 | 의태어 | sneaking quietly |
| 뒤죽박죽 | 의태어 | jumbled, messy |
| 방긋 | 의태어 | smiling brightly |
| 훌쩍훌쩍 | mixed | sniffling while crying |
| 쿨쿨 | 의성어 | snoring/deep sleep |
| 데굴데굴 | 의태어 | rolling around |

These typically function as adverbs, often doubled for repeated action, and are paired with 하다 to form verbs.

## 6. Common Mistakes for English/Dutch Speakers

### Grammar Mistakes

- **은/는 vs 이/가 misuse:** Defaulting to 는 everywhere — leads to unnatural discourse. Rule: new information or answers to "who/what" want 이/가.
- **Forcing English word order:** Trying to keep subject-verb adjacency — Korean's SOV structure with heavy pronoun-dropping means literal translation is often wrong.
- **Overusing pronouns:** 저는, 그는, 그녀는 — Korean drops subjects/objects far more than English. Constant pronoun use sounds stilted.
- **Direct translation of "to have":** English "I have a car" ≠ 나는 자동차를 가지다. Korean prefers 저는 자동차가 있어요 (literally "a car exists for me").

### Speech Level Mistakes

- **Ignoring speech levels:** Using 반말 with someone older or in professional context is a real social error, not optional politeness — treat 존댓말/반말 choice as grammatically mandatory.

### Particle & Verb Mistakes

- **Confusing 안 vs 못:** 안 = simple negation ("don't"), 못 = inability ("can't"). English "can't" means both.
- **Literal article translation:** Korean has no articles — don't try to insert 이/그 as article equivalents; they're demonstratives ("this/that").
- **Skipping batchim pronunciation rules:** Reading Hangul letter-by-letter without applying 연음/비음화/경음화 produces speech that's technically correct but doesn't sound like real Korean.

### Vocabulary & Pragmatics

- **Treating 좀 as always meaning "a little":** 좀 is a softener in requests (사진 좀 찍어 주세요, "please take a photo") — it doesn't always carry its literal meaning.
- **Assuming 있다/없다 honorific:** The honorific form of 있다 (for people) is suppletive 계시다, not 있으시다.

## 8. Cultural & Pragmatic Notes

### 8.1 Age-Hierarchy Culture Drives Grammar

Korean social structure (rooted in Confucian tradition) means age and social position determine speech level far more rigidly than in Dutch/English. It's normal to ask someone's age/birth year early in a relationship to establish the correct register — this isn't rude in Korean context, it's functionally necessary for grammatically correct speech.

### 8.2 반말 (Casual Speech) Signals Relationship Status

Switching to 반말 (말을 놓다, "to drop the formal speech") is often an explicit, negotiated moment ("말 놓을까요?") rather than something that happens automatically. Doing it unprompted with someone older is a genuine social misstep.

### 8.3 Business/Email Conventions

- Default to 합니다체 or 해요체 — never 반말
- Opening: 안녕하세요, [회사명]의 [이름]입니다.
- Closing: 감사합니다, 잘 부탁드립니다 (unique phrase with no English equivalent — appears in nearly all professional correspondence)
- Address by title/position (부장님, 과장님) rather than first name, especially up the hierarchy

### 8.4 눈치 (Nunchi) — Reading the Room

Culturally central concept: the ability to read unspoken social cues and adjust behavior/speech accordingly. Explains why indirectness, hedging (좀, -것 같다), and avoiding direct refusal are common pragmatic strategies.

### 8.5 Indirect Refusal Patterns

Direct "no" (아니요) can feel abrupt. Softer alternatives:
- **좀 어려울 것 같아요** ("that seems a bit difficult" = polite no)
- **생각해 볼게요** ("I'll think about it" — often a soft decline)
- **다음에요** ("next time" — frequently a polite deferral)
