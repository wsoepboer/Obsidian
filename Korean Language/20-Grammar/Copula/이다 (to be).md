# The Copula: 이다 (to be)

The copula 이다 equates two nouns and is the Korean "to be" for noun + noun sentences.

---

## Present Tense

### Polite informal (해요체)

| After consonant | After vowel |
|---|---|
| N + **이에요** | N + **예요** |

**Examples**:
- 학생이에요. (I am a student.)
- 의사예요. (He/she is a doctor.)
- 비싸요? → No, use 비싼 adjective, not copula

### Formal polite (합쇼체)

- N + **입니다** (after consonant)
- N + **입니다** (after vowel; same form)
- 학생입니다. (I am a student. — formal)

### Plain/written form

- N + **이다**
- 학생이다. (dictionary/written form)

---

## Negation: 아니다

**Pattern**: N + **이/가 아니에요** (subject particle required!)

- 학생이 아니에요. (I am not a student.)
- 의사가 아니에요. (He/she is not a doctor.)

**Not**: ~~학생이 아니에요~~ (missing the 이/가 — grammatically incomplete, though common in speech)

Formal: N + 이/가 + **아닙니다**
- 학생이 아닙니다.

---

## Past Tense

### Polite informal

- N + **였어요**
- 학생이었어요. (I was a student.)
- 의사였어요. (He/she was a doctor.)

### Formal

- N + **였습니다**

---

## Comparison with Adjectives

The copula 이다 is unique — it equates nouns. Adjectives (descriptive verbs) describe qualities and follow different rules.

| | Copula (이다) | Adjective (e.g., 크다) |
|---|---|---|
| **Dictionary form** | 이다 | 크다 |
| **Present polite** | 이에요 / 입니다 | 커요 / 큽니다 |
| **Use** | N + 이다 (noun predicates) | Describes qualities |
| **Example** | 학생이에요 (is a student) | 커요 (is big) |

---

## Subject Honorifics with 이다

To show respect toward the subject, insert -시- before the copula:

- 할아버지께서 선생님이세요. (Grandfather is a teacher. — respectful toward grandfather)
