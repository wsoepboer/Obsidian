# Directional Particles: 에게 / 한테

Marks the **target** of an action — the person to whom something is given, said, sent, done.

> **Terminology note**: in these notes 에게/한테 is the **action-target marker**, not an "indirect object marker". It marks *who the action is aimed at*, and it happily co-occurs with the object particle 을/를 in the same sentence — see the section below on 에게/한테 + 을/를. Calling it an indirect-object marker suggests the two compete; they don't.

---

## Two Options

### 에게
**More written / neutral** — textbooks, letters, formal speech.

### 한테
**More spoken / casual** — everyday conversation.

Both are correct and interchangeable in meaning; choose based on register. There is **no 받침 rule** — the form never changes.

| Base | 받침 | + 에게 | + 한테 |
|------|------|--------|--------|
| 동생 | O | 동생**에게** | 동생**한테** |
| 학생 | O | 학생**에게** | 학생**한테** |
| 누나 | X | 누나**에게** | 누나**한테** |
| 오빠 | X | 오빠**에게** | 오빠**한테** |

**Honorific**: to an elder or superior, use **께** — 선생님**께** 편지를 써요, 할머니**께** 선물을 드려요 (with 드리다, the humble form of 주다).

---

## Pattern

**Noun(person) + 에게/한테 + Action**

---

## Key Examples

### Giving/Sending
- 아버지**한테** 선물을 줘요.
  (I give a gift to my father.)
- 할머니**에게** 카드를 보내요.
  (I send a card to my grandmother.)
- 저는 친구**에게** 생일 문자를 보내요.
  (I send a birthday text to a friend.)

### Calling/Writing
- 엄마**가** 저**한테** 전화를 해요.
  (Mom calls me.)
- 친구**한테** 편지를 써요.
  (I write a letter to my friend.)

### Expressing to someone
- 선생님**에게** 질문을 해요.
  (I ask a question to my teacher.)

---

## With a direct object (에게/한테 + 을/를)

A sentence can carry **both** an action target (에게/한테) and a direct object (을/를) at once. They mark different things, so they never replace each other.

**Pattern**: Noun(target) + 에게/한테 + Noun(thing) + 을/를 + Verb

- 동생**에게** 책**을** 줘요. / 동생**한테** 책**을** 줘요.
  (I give a book to my younger sibling.)
- 나타샤 씨는 친구**에게** 편지**를** 써요.
  (Natasha writes a letter to her friend.)
- 프엉 씨가 저**한테** 선물**을** 줬어요.
  (Phuong gave me a gift. — past: 주다 → 줬어요)

Word order is flexible as long as the verb stays last: 저는 동생에게 책을 줘요 = 저는 책을 동생에게 줘요. The target-before-object order is the neutral one.

## 에게/한테 (person) vs. 에 (place)

Both come out as "to" in English, but Korean keeps them apart:

| Particle | Target | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **에게/한테** | a **person** | 친구**에게** 전화를 해요 (I call my friend) |
| **에** | a **place** | 학교**에** 가요 (I go to school) |

- ❌ 친구**에** 전화를 해요.
- ✓ 친구**에게/한테** 전화를 해요.
- ❌ 학교**에게** 가요.
- ✓ 학교**에** 가요.

See: [[20-Grammar/Particles/Location (에)|Location Particle 에]]

## Getting things *from* a person: 에게서 / 한테서

Add 서 to flip the direction — the person becomes the **source**.

- 친구**한테서** 편지를 받았어요. (I got a letter from a friend.)
- 선생님**에게서** 카드를 받아요. (I receive a card from my teacher.)

In speech, plain 한테 often covers "from" too when the verb makes it obvious (친구한테 받았어요). This mirrors [[20-Grammar/Particles/Action Location (에서)|에서]], which is the place version of "from".

## Quick Reference

| Action | Example |
|--------|---------|
| Give | 부모님**에게** 선물을 줘요 |
| Send | 친구**한테** 문자를 보내요 |
| Call | 누나**에게** 전화해요 |
| Write | 할머니**한테** 편지를 써요 |
| Receive (from) | 친구**한테서** 선물을 받아요 |
| To an elder | 선생님**께** 질문을 해요 |

## Practice

**Fill in 에게 or 한테** (both work — note which register you picked):

1. 가영 씨가 리사 씨\_\_\_ 전화를 해요.
   - Answer: 가영 씨가 리사 씨**에게/한테** 전화를 해요.
2. 나타샤 씨는 친구\_\_\_ 편지를 써요.
   - Answer: 나타샤 씨는 친구**에게/한테** 편지를 써요.
3. 프엉 씨가 저\_\_\_ 선물을 줬어요.
   - Answer: 프엉 씨가 저**에게/한테** 선물을 줬어요.
4. 저는 동생\_\_\_ 책을 줘요.
   - Answer: 동생**에게/한테** (직접목적어 책**을** stays)
5. 선생님\_\_\_ 편지를 써요. (to a teacher)
   - Answer: 선생님**께** — honorific
