# Topic & Subject Particles: 은/는 vs. 이/가

Two particles mark subject-like roles differently in Korean.

---

## Topic Particle: 은/는

**Rule**: 받침 O → 은 | 받침 X → 는

**Usage**: Introduces a **topic** or makes a **general statement**

**Examples**:
- 사과**는** 과일이에요.
  (Apples are fruit. — *general statement*)
- 저**는** 학생입니다.
  (I'm a student. — *introducing myself*)
- 오늘**은** 날씨가 좋아요.
  (Today the weather is good. — *topic shift*)

**Key point**: Emphasizes the broader context or introduces what you're talking about.

---

## Subject Particle: 이/가

**Rule**: 받침 O → 이 | 받침 X → 가

**Usage**: Points out a **specific subject** or answers "who?" / "what?"

**Examples**:
- 사과**가** 좋아요.
  (Apples are good. — *pointing out apples specifically*)
- 누가 왔어요? → 철수**가** 왔어요.
  (Who came? → Cheolsu came. — *answering specifically*)
- 오늘 날씨**가** 좋아요.
  (Today's weather is good. — *emphasizing the weather itself*)

**Key point**: Marks specific, pointed-out subjects.

---

## Quick Comparison

| | 은/는 | 이/가 |
|---|---|---|
| **When to use** | General topics, introductions | Specific subjects, answers to "who?" |
| **Emphasis** | Context/broader setting | The actual subject itself |
| **Feel** | Softer, more conversational | Sharper, more specific |
| **Example** | 저**는** 한국어를 배워요 (I learn Korean — general statement) | 누가 한국어를 배워요? 저**가** 배워요 (Who learns Korean? I do — specific answer) |

## Practice

**Fill in 은/는 or 이/가**:

1. 고양이\_\_\_ 귀엽습니다. (Cats are cute.)
   - Answer: 고양이**가** 귀엽습니다 (emphasizing cats)

2. 저\_\_\_ 한국에 가요. (I go to Korea.)
   - Answer: 저**는** 한국에 가요 (general statement)
