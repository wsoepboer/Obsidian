# Numbers & Time (숫자와 시간)

Korean uses **two entirely different number systems** for different purposes, and **requires counters** to count most things.

---

## The Two Number Systems

| System | Use | Examples | Notes |
|--------|-----|----------|-------|
| **Native Korean** | Counting objects, people, age (casual), hours | 한 개 (1), 세 명 (3 people), 여덟 살 (8 years old), 세 시 (3 o'clock) | 하나, 둘, 셋, 넷... (up to 99 only) |
| **Sino-Korean** | Dates, money, phone numbers, minutes, floors | 일일 (1st), 오천원 (5,000 won), 십분 (10 min), 삼층 (3rd floor) | 일, 이, 삼, 사... (1–99 and above) |

---

## Key Rule: Mixing Systems

In a single time expression, you naturally **mix both systems** — this is standard:

- **지금 세 시 삼십 분이에요.** (It's 3:30 now.)
  - 세 시 = 3 o'clock (native hour + counter 시)
  - 삼십 분 = 30 minutes (Sino-Korean number + counter 분)

## Core Sections

### [[20-Grammar/Numbers & Time/Native Korean Numbers|Native Korean Numbers]]
- Numbers 1–20: 하나, 둘, 셋, 넷, 다섯, 여섯, 일곱, 여덟, 아홉, 열...
- Tens (20–90): 스물, 서른, 마흔, 쉰, 예순, 일흔, 여든, 아흔
- Numbers up to 99 (combines 10s + ones: 스물다섯 = 25)
- Shortened forms before counters: 한, 두, 세, 네, 스무
- **Used for**: hours, age (casual), counting objects/people/animals

### [[20-Grammar/Numbers & Time/Sino-Korean Numbers|Sino-Korean Numbers]]
- Digits 0–10: 일, 이, 삼, 사, 오, 육, 칠, 팔, 구, 십
- Place values: 백 (100), 천 (1,000), 만 (10,000), 백만 (1 million), 억 (100 million)
- **Used for**: dates, money, phone numbers, minutes, floors, large numbers (100+)

### [[20-Grammar/Numbers & Time/📌 Counters (단위 명사)|Counters]]
- **Essential**: Every count requires a counter word (unit noun)
- Common counters: 개 (objects), 명 (people), 마리 (animals), 권 (books), 장 (paper), 잔 (cups), 시간 (hours of duration)
- Pattern: Noun + **Number + Counter**
- Shortened numbers before counters: 한 개, 두 명, 세 살, 네 시

## Time of Day

| Time | Korean | System |
|------|--------|--------|
| 1 o'clock | 한 시 | Native (hour) |
| 3:30 | 세 시 삼십 분 | Native (hour) + Sino (minutes) |
| 10:45 | 열 시 사십오 분 | Native (hour) + Sino (minutes) |

**Rule**: Always use **native Korean for hours**; **Sino-Korean for minutes**.

---

## Dates

| Date | Korean | System |
|------|--------|--------|
| March 5th | 삼월 오일 | Sino-Korean (both month & date) |
| 2026년 8월 1일 | 이천이십육년, 팔월, 일일 | Sino-Korean (all) |
| June 25th | 육월 이십오일 | Sino-Korean |

**Rule**: Always use **Sino-Korean for months and dates**.

## Age

| Age | Korean | System | Context |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 years old | 다섯 살 | Native | Casual, everyday speech |
| 20 years old | 스물 살 | Native | Casual, everyday speech |
| 25 years of age | 이십오 세 | Sino | Formal, official documents |

## Duration

| Duration | Korean | System |
|---|---|---|
| 2 hours | 두 시간 | Native number |
| 30 minutes | 삼십 분 | Sino number |
| 3 months | 삼 개월 (or 세 달) | Sino (개월) or Native (달) |
| 1 year | 일 년 | Sino number |

## Quick Decision Guide

**Choose NATIVE KOREAN when:**
- Counting objects/people/animals (with counter)
- Expressing age in casual conversation
- Telling clock hours
- Counting something up to 99

**Choose SINO-KOREAN when:**
- Writing/speaking dates (year, month, day)
- Mentioning minutes, seconds
- Discussing money
- Giving floor numbers, room numbers
- Saying formal age (세, official documents)
- Any number 100 or above
- Phone numbers, addresses
