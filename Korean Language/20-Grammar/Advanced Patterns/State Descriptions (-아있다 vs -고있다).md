# State Descriptions: -아/어있다 vs. -고있다

Two ways to describe a state, with different meanings:
- **-고있다** = ongoing action right now
- **-아/어있다** = resultant state (stable condition from a completed action)

---

## -고있다 (현재 진행: Present Progressive)

**Pattern**: Verb stem + **-고 있다** → "is/are doing X right now"

Used for an **action in progress** at the moment of speaking.

- **먹고 있어요**. (I **am eating** right now.)
- **공부하고 있어요**. (I **am studying** right now.)
- **뛰고 있어요**. (I **am running** right now.)
- 영화를 **보고 있어요**. (I **am watching** a movie right now.)

**Feeling/emotion verbs also use -고있다:**
- 슬프고 있어요. (I am feeling sad. — momentary)
- 화나고 있어요. (I am angry. — right now, possibly temporary)

---

## -아/어있다 (상태: Resultant State)

**Pattern**: Verb stem + **-아/어 있다** → the result of a completed action that continues

Used for a **stable state or condition**, usually following a verb of motion or change.

**Vowel harmony**: ㅏ/ㅗ vowel → -아, else → -어

- **앉아 있어요**. (I **am seated**. — sitting as a result of having sat down)
- **누워 있어요**. (I **am lying down**. — lying as a result of having lain down)
- 문이 **열어 있어요**. (The door **is open**. — open as a result of having opened)
- 책이 **놓여 있어요**. (The book **is placed** [on the table]. — in a placed state)
- 불이 **켜져 있어요**. (The light **is on**. — on as a result of having turned on)

**Key characteristic**: The state is presented as a **completed result** that persists.

---

## Critical Difference

### 먹고 있어요 (eating right now)
- Focus: the **action happening now**
- Duration: short-term, momentary
- Implication: still actively engaged in the action

### 먹어 있어요 (not common; unusual phrasing)
- Would mean: "is in a state of having eaten" (not a natural thing to say about eating)
- -아/어있다 doesn't work naturally with simple consumption verbs

---

## Verbs That Work Best with Each

### Better with -고있다
- 먹다 (eat)
- 마시다 (drink)
- 공부하다 (study)
- 일하다 (work)
- 보다 (watch/see)
- 듣다 (listen)
- 생각하다 (think)
- 기다리다 (wait)

### Better with -아/어있다
- 앉다 (sit) → 앉아있어요 (I'm seated)
- 서다 (stand) → 서 있어요 (I'm standing)
- 누우다 (lie) → 누워있어요 (I'm lying down)
- 열다 (open) → 열어있어요 (It's open)
- 닫다 (close) → 닫혀있어요 (It's closed)
- 켜다 (turn on) → 켜져있어요 (It's on)
- 끄다 (turn off) → 꺼져있어요 (It's off)
- 놓다 (place) → 놓여있어요 (It's placed)
- 걸다 (hang) → 걸려있어요 (It's hanging)

**Pattern**: Verbs of position, change of state, or placement naturally use -아/어있다.

---

## Examples in Context

### -고있다 (ongoing action)
- 지금 **전화하고 있어요**. (I'm on the phone right now.)
- 비가 **오고 있어요**. (It's raining right now.)
- 아이들이 **뛰어놀고 있어요**. (The kids are running around playing.)

### -아/어있다 (resultant state)
- 문이 **잠겨 있어요**. (The door is locked.)
- 창이 **열려 있어요**. (The window is open.)
- 그 사람이 **여기 있어요**. (That person is here — standing/present.)
- 책이 테이블 위에 **놓여 있어요**. (The book is placed on the table.)

---

## With Emotions (Edge Case)

Emotions can use either, with slightly different nuances:

- 화나고 있어요. (I **am getting angry** / **am angry right now**. — focus on the feeling happening)
- 화나 있어요. (I **am in an angry state**. — less common, more emphasizing the stable state)

For most emotions, -고있다 is more natural.

---

## Practice

**Choose -고있다 or -아/어있다**:

1. 지금 밥\_\_\_. (I'm eating rice right now.)
   - Answer: 먹고 있어요 (ongoing action)

2. 불\_\_\_? (Is the light on?)
   - Answer: 켜져 있어요? (state of being on)

3. 숙제를 \_\_\_. (I'm doing homework.)
   - Answer: 하고 있어요 (action in progress)

4. 우산이 구석에 \_\_\_. (The umbrella is leaning in the corner.)
   - Answer: 놓여 있어요 (resultant position)
