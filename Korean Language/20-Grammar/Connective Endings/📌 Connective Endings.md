# Connective Endings (연결 어미)

Join two clauses by attaching to the first verb/adjective stem. These replace English conjunctions ("and," "because," "if," "but," etc.).

---

## Common Connective Endings

### -고 ("and" — simple sequence/listing)

Combines two actions or states without implying cause-effect.

- 밥을 먹**고** 학교에 가요. (I eat and (then) go to school.)
- 날씨가 맑**고** 따뜻해요. (The weather is clear and warm.)
- 책을 읽**고** 숙제를 해요. (I read a book and do homework.)

**Note**: -고 implies sequence (one after the other) but NOT necessarily that one causes the other.

---

### -아서 / -어서 ("because, so" — cause, or sequence with result)

Same vowel-harmony rule as regular conjugation (ㅏ/ㅗ → 아서, else → 어서).

Used when the **first clause causes or leads to the second clause**.

- 배**가** 고파**서** 먹었어요. (I was hungry, **so** I ate.)
- 비가 와**서** 밖에 안 나가요. (It rains, **so** I don't go outside.)
- 피곤해**서** 쉬었어요. (I was tired, **so** I rested.)

**Critical limitation**: Cannot be followed by a **command or suggestion** (no imperatives). Use -(으)니까 for that.

---

### -(으)니까 ("because, since" — reason, often followed by command/suggestion)

Can be followed by commands, suggestions, or imperative sentences.

- 늦었**으니까** 서두르세요. (Since it's late, **hurry up**.) ← command OK
- 배고프**니까** 밥을 드세요. (Since you're hungry, **please eat**.) ← suggestion OK
- 시간이 없**으니까** 가야 해요. (Since there's no time, (we) have to go.)

**Vowel harmony**: 받침 O → -으니까, 받침 X → -니까
- 먹다 → 먹으니까
- 가다 → 가니까

---

### -지만 ("but, although" — contrast)

Introduces a contrasting second clause.

- 비싸**지만** 좋아요. (It's expensive, **but** it's good.)
- 작**지만** 예뻐요. (It's small, **but** it's pretty.)
- 늦었**지만** 왔어요. (It was late, **but** (I) came.)

---

### -(으)면 ("if" — conditional)

Vowel harmony: 받침 O → -으면, 받침 X → -면

- 비가 오**면** 안 가요. (If it rains, I won't go.)
- 시간이 있**으면** 올게요. (If I have time, I'll come.)
- 공부하**면** 합격할 거예요. (If (you) study, (you'll) pass.)

---

### -는데 / -(으)ㄴ데 ("and/but" — background setup for next statement)

Introduces context or a contrary situation leading into the next clause. Less direct than -지만.

- 배고픈**데** 밥이 없어요. (I'm hungry, **but** there's no food.)
- 날씨가 좋은**데** 우산을 가져갈까요? (The weather's nice, **but** should I take an umbrella?)

**Usage**: Often trails off or leads to a question; suggests "given this situation, what about [next clause]?"

---

### -(으)려고 ("in order to, intending to" — purpose)

Shows the **purpose or intention** of an action.

- 공부하**려고** 도서관에 가요. (I go to the library **in order to study**.)
- 쉬**려고** 앉았어요. (I sat down **to rest**.)
- 돈을 벌**려고** 일해요. (I work **in order to earn money**.)

---

### -(으)러 ("in order to" — purpose, motion verbs only)

Like -(으)려고, but used **only with verbs of motion** (가다, 오다, 내려가다, 올라가다, etc.).

- 밥을 먹**으러** 가요. (I'm going **to eat**.)
- 친구를 만나**러** 왔어요. (I came **to meet my friend**.)
- 쇼핑하**러** 갈래요? (Do you want to go **shopping**?)

**Difference from -(으)려고**: -(으)러 is more common with motion and shorter/simpler phrases.

---

### -(으)면서 ("while" — simultaneous/concurrent actions)

Two actions happening at the same time.

- 음악을 들**으면서** 공부해요. (I study **while listening to music**.)
- 웃**으면서** 말해요. (He speaks **while laughing**.)
- TV를 보**면서** 밥을 먹어요. (I eat **while watching TV**.)

---

## Speech Level with Connective Endings

The **first clause** (before the connective ending) uses the **plain/stem form** (no politeness ending on the first verb). The **second clause's final verb** sets the overall politeness level:

- 배고파**서** 밥을 먹어요. (1st: plain 배고파서 + 2nd: polite 먹어요)
- 배고파**서** 밥을 먹습니다. (1st: plain 배고파서 + 2nd: formal 먹습니다)

---

## Common Mistakes

### ❌ Using -아서/어서 before a command
- ❌ 배고파**서** 먹으세요.
- ✓ 배고프**니까** 드세요. (use -(으)니까 for commands)

### ❌ Using -(으)러 with non-motion verbs
- ❌ 공부하**러** 한다. (공부하다 is not a motion verb)
- ✓ 공부하**려고** 한다. (use -(으)려고)

### ❌ Wrong vowel harmony for -(으)면
- ❌ 가으면 (should be 가면)
- ✓ 가**면** (받침 X → -면)
- ✓ 먹**으면** (받침 O → -으면)
